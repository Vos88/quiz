4. "Mad-Eye Moody" (actually Barty Crouch Jr. via Polyjuice all year)

7. *(no school year — on the run / Snape made Headmaster under Voldemort's regime, per book 7)*

## Central antagonist / plot device by book
1. Quirrell/Voldemort after the Philosopher's Stone
2. Tom Riddle's diary (an early, unnamed Horcrux) + the Basilisk; secondary antagonist Lucius Malfoy
3. Sirius Black wrongly blamed; real traitor is Peter Pettigrew, hiding as Ron's rat Scabbers
4. Barty Crouch Jr. rigs the Triwizard Tournament to resurrect Voldemort via Harry's blood; Cedric Diggory dies
5. Dolores Umbridge's Ministry-backed authoritarian takeover of Hogwarts runs in parallel with Voldemort's scheme to retrieve a prophecy about Harry, luring him to the Department of Mysteries; Sirius Black dies in the ensuing battle
6. The Horcrux hunt intertwines with the "Half-Blood Prince" mystery (young Snape); Dumbledore is killed by Snape at the climax
7. Hunt for and destruction of all 7 Horcruxes + the three Deathly Hallows; Battle of Hogwarts; Voldemort dies when his own curse rebounds off the Elder Wand

1. Gryffindor wins the House Cup (last-minute points from Dumbledore); Harry's first year as Seeker
2. *(see book2_notes.md for details — not yet copied into this ledger)*

## Weasley family roster (as introduced/developed)

- Ron — Harry's best friend, eventually Gryffindor Keeper and Prefect
- Ginny — youngest, starts Hogwarts in book 2; becomes a Gryffindor Chaser/Seeker; romance with Harry from book 6
- Mr & Mrs Weasley — parents; family nine-handed clock shows members' status/location/peril

- Book 5: Sirius Black (falls through the Veil in the Department of Mysteries, hit by Bellatrix Lestrange's curse)

## Running gags / callbacks (good "earlier-book" trivia for later quizzes)

- "Constant vigilance" — Moody's catchphrase (book 4), ironic once he's revealed as an impostor

## Dumbledore's Army (book 5) — good source for a franchise "name the members" round

## Franchise-wide patterns (cross-book analysis)

Found by re-reading all 7 fact sheets specifically for recurring structure/theme, not just per-book facts. Each pattern below is evidence-backed per book; where a pattern doesn't actually hold, that's stated rather than forced — useful material for a "spot the pattern" or "odd one out" style franchise round.

**New DADA teacher every single year, confirmed in-story as "jinxed."** Quirrell (1) → Lockhart (2) → Lupin (3) → "Moody"/Crouch Jr. (4) → Umbridge (5) → Snape (6) → Amycus Carrow (7, reported secondhand — the trio isn't enrolled that year). The books themselves lampshade this: book 3 ch1 calls Lupin the trio's "third DADA teacher," reputedly jinxed; book 5 ch9 has Harry tally it up — "one sacked, one dead, one's memory removed, one locked in a trunk for nine months."

**Opening structure splits cleanly in two.** Books 1, 2, 3, 5 open their first chapter on Harry at Privet Drive. Books 4, 6, 7 instead open with a non-Harry "villain-side" cold-open chapter (the Riddle House; the Other Minister/Spinner's End; the Dark Lord Ascending at Malfoy Manor) before cutting to Harry. A real evolution toward darker, Voldemort's-eye-view openings as the series progresses — good "which book does this chapter open" round material.

**Year-end feast/celebration holds, then is subverted, then abandoned.** Standard House Cup feast: books 1–3. Book 5: Harry skips it outright. Book 4: subverted — black mourning drapes for Cedric instead of house colours, no Cup awarded. Book 6: dropped entirely — Dumbledore's funeral replaces it. Book 7: no Hogwarts year exists in the main story at all.

**Escalating death toll: 0 → 0 → 0 → 1 → 1 → 1 → many.** Books 1–3 have zero on-page deaths (each book's own notes confirm this explicitly). Then one significant named death per book: Cedric Diggory (4), Sirius Black (5), Dumbledore (6) — before book 7's mass Battle of Hogwarts casualties (Fred Weasley, Lupin, Tonks, Colin Creevey, Snape, Moody, Dobby, Bellatrix, Crabbe, Voldemort, plus ~50 unnamed). A clean, quiz-friendly escalation.

**Quidditch absent exactly twice, both explained in-text.** Present with a full inter-house season in books 1, 2, 3, 5, 6. Absent in book 4 (Dumbledore cancels the House Cup in favour of the Triwizard Tournament) and book 7 (no Hogwarts year for the trio at all).

**Snape-as-suspected-villain red herring — strong in 4 of 6 applicable books, weak in 2.** Solid in books 1 (wrongly suspected of pursuing the Stone), 4 (falsely named a Death Eater under Veritaserum in a Pensieve memory), 5 (openly distrusted, defended only by Dumbledore), and 6→7 (seemingly confirmed by Dumbledore's murder, then fully reversed by "The Prince's Tale"). The pattern is notably thin in books 2 and 3, where other characters (Hagrid/Malfoy, Black/Pettigrew) carry the red-herring role instead — don't force a Snape-suspicion question onto those two books.

**Trio internal friction recurs every book, with an escalating trigger each time.** Minor Ron/Hermione froideur mended by the troll (1) → Hermione reports Harry's Firebolt, he's furious (3) → Ron doesn't believe Harry didn't enter the Tournament himself (4) → Harry's quiet isolation over being passed over as prefect (5) → Ron and Hermione stop speaking over Lavender Brown (6) → Ron has a bitter row with Harry and actually Disapparates away, abandoning the group (7, Godric's Hollow chapter) — the only book where a trio member leaves outright.

**Malfoy's role escalates from schoolboy rival to reluctant near-defector.** Steals Neville's Remembrall and tricks Harry into a fake duel (1) → becomes Slytherin Seeker via his father's money, Lucius is revealed as the diary's planter (2) → fakes an injury, disguises as a dementor to sabotage a match, gets slapped by Hermione (3) → transfigured into a ferret by fake-Moody (4) → Inquisitorial Squad member, confiscates Harry's wand (5) → given Voldemort's actual assassination mission, ultimately can't kill Dumbledore himself ("you are not a killer") (6) → at Malfoy Manor, falsely claims he can't identify a captured Harry — his first on-page act of mercy (7).

**Recurring objects with explicit, notes-confirmed later payoffs.** The Marauder's Map (introduced book 3, reused books 4–7). The Invisibility Cloak (given book 1, eventually confirmed as a genuine Deathly Hallow and kept rather than returned, book 7). The Sword of Gryffindor absorbing Basilisk venom when it kills the Basilisk in book 2 — explicitly the reason it can destroy Horcruxes, confirmed in book 7 ch16. Fred and George's joke-shop arc: pranks (2) → funded by Harry's Triwizard winnings (4) → shop opens (5) → supplies Order/resistance gear like Shield Hats and Instant Darkness Powder during the occupation (6–7).

**The number seven pays off almost entirely in book 7 itself**, rather than being seeded evenly through the series: Voldemort's soul split into 7 pieces (6 intentional Horcruxes + the fragment in his body, with Harry as the accidental extra); "The Seven Potters" decoy operation; the Deathly Hallows tied to the Tale of the Three Brothers; 17 as the age of magical majority, heavily marked in book 7. Good "which book" trivia, but not a clue that pays off earlier.

**Patterns checked and explicitly rejected rather than forced:**
- *"Final challenge happens somewhere hidden/underground"* — true for books 1 (trapdoor), 2 (Chamber), and 5 (Department of Mysteries), but clearly false for 3 (Shrieking Shack/open grounds), 4 (an open graveyard reached by Portkey), and 7 (Forbidden Forest/Great Hall — "forbidden" in name only). Don't present this as a universal rule.
- *Title-object naming timing* — no consistent "always early" or "always late" reveal: ranges from the first chapter (Sirius Black, book 3) to the very last chapter (the Half-Blood Prince's identity, book 6). Character/organisation titles (3, 5) reveal immediately; object titles (1, 2, 4, 7) reveal mid-to-late. Not a clean single rule, but a decent "early vs. late revealer" two-way split question.
- Harry's exact on-page birthday age is confirmed in books 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7 but book 4's notes don't contain an explicit "turns 14" line — flagged as a sourcing gap in book4_notes.md, not a broken pattern.
