## Chapter 1: The Dark Lord Ascending

**Plot events (in order):**
- Severus Snape and Yaxley meet on a moonlit lane and walk to Malfoy Manor, passing through enchanted gates and a yew hedge (a white peacock is seen on the hedge).
- They join a long table of Death Eaters in the drawing room; a prisoner hangs upside down, revolving, over the table.
- Voldemort questions Yaxley and Snape about plans to move Harry Potter from Privet Drive; Snape reveals the Order plans to move Harry "Saturday next, at nightfall," from an inside source; Yaxley has heard a conflicting date (the thirtieth) from the Imperiused Auror Dawlish — a deliberate false trail.
- Yaxley reports he has placed an Imperius Curse on Pius Thicknesse, Head of the Department of Magical Law Enforcement, to help subvert the Ministry.
- Voldemort states he must personally kill Harry and asks to borrow a wand (since his and Harry's wands share a core/connection). Lucius Malfoy is forced to surrender his wand (elm, dragon heartstring core); Narcissa secretly signals him to comply.
- Voldemort mocks the Malfoys for their unhappiness; reveals that Nymphadora Tonks (niece of Bellatrix and Narcissa) has married the werewolf Remus Lupin, provoking mocking laughter; Bellatrix disowns her.
- Voldemort asks Draco if he'll "babysit the cubs" (mocking).
- Nagini, Voldemort's giant snake, is described (neck as thick as a man's thigh) and climbs onto his shoulders.
- The captive is revealed as Charity Burbage, the recently "resigned" Muggle Studies teacher at Hogwarts, condemned for writing in the Daily Prophet in defense of Muggle-borns.
- Voldemort kills Charity Burbage with the Killing Curse ("Avada Kedavra"); her body falls onto the table.
- Voldemort feeds her corpse to Nagini: "Dinner, Nagini."

**Named characters:** Severus Snape, Yaxley, Voldemort (Lord Voldemort/Tom Riddle, unnamed directly here but referred to as "he"), Lucius Malfoy, Narcissa Malfoy, Draco Malfoy, Bellatrix Lestrange, Peter Pettigrew ("Wormtail," keeping a prisoner quiet below the floor — later revealed to be someone else imprisoned in the cellar, though at this point just referenced as making noise), Dolohov (described as having a long, twisted face), Charity Burbage (Muggle Studies professor), Nagini (the snake), Remus Lupin and Tonks (mentioned as newlyweds, not present).

**Places/objects/spells:** Malfoy Manor (drawing room, marble mantelpiece, gilded mirror); wands — Lucius's elm wand with dragon heartstring core; the Killing Curse (Avada Kedavra); mention of the Order of the Phoenix; Azkaban (implied via Death Eaters' free movement); Daily Prophet (Charity's article on Mudbloods).

**Numbers/specifics:** Harry to be moved "Saturday next, at nightfall" per Snape's source; Dawlish's false information says the move is on "the thirtieth," the night before Harry's seventeenth birthday (31st). Lucius's wand: elm wood, dragon heartstring core.

**Obscure details:** A white peacock struts on the hedge at Malfoy Manor. The portraits' eyes follow Snape and Yaxley down the hallway. One squat Death Eater gives a "wheezy giggle." Voldemort's eyes are red with vertical slit pupils; his skin has a "pearly glow."

## Chapter 2: In Memoriam

**Plot events:**
- Harry, at Privet Drive, cuts his finger on a shard of his two-way mirror (gift from Sirius Black) while clearing out his school trunk.
- He finds in the trunk debris: an old "Support Cedric Diggory"/"Potter Stinks" badge, a cracked Sneakoscope, and the fake locket Horcrux with the note signed "R.A.B." inside.
- He packs a rucksack with his Invisibility Cloak, Marauder's Map, potion kit, photo album from Hagrid, letters, and his wand.
- He reads Elphias Doge's Daily Prophet obituary of Albus Dumbledore: they met at age eleven; Dumbledore's father Percival was convicted of attacking three Muggle boys and died in Azkaban; Dumbledore became the top student, corresponded with Nicolas Flamel, Bathilda Bagshot, and Adalbert Waffling; discovered twelve uses of dragon's blood; his brother Aberforth arrived three years later; their mother Kendra died, and then sister Ariana died shortly after, causing a rift between Albus and Aberforth; the obituary references the famous 1945 duel between Dumbledore and Gellert Grindelwald.
- Harry reads a teaser in the same Prophet for Rita Skeeter's upcoming biography "The Life and Lies of Albus Dumbledore," interviewed by Betty Braithwaite: claims Dumbledore dabbled in the Dark Arts, that Ivor Dillonsby claims to have discovered eight uses of dragon's blood before Dumbledore "borrowed" his work (detailed in "chapter sixteen" of Skeeter's book), teases revelations about Dumbledore's mother and sister ("chapters nine to twelve"), hints the Grindelwald duel wasn't as it seemed, and an entire chapter on the "Potter-Dumbledore relationship."
- Harry is enraged, throws the paper away.
- He glimpses what he thinks is a flash of blue (like Dumbledore's eye) in the mirror shard, but dismisses it as imagination.

**Named characters:** Harry Potter, Albus Dumbledore (deceased, subject of obituary), Elphias Doge (obituary author, Special Advisor to the Wizengamot), Percival Dumbledore (Albus's father), Kendra Dumbledore (Albus's mother, deceased), Ariana Dumbledore (Albus's sister, deceased), Aberforth Dumbledore (Albus's brother), Nicolas Flamel, Bathilda Bagshot, Adalbert Waffling, Gellert Grindelwald, Rita Skeeter, Betty Braithwaite, Hedwig (Harry's owl, sulking), Sirius Black (mentioned, deceased godfather), Ivor Dillonsby (mentioned by Skeeter).

**Objects/places:** Number four, Privet Drive; two-way mirror shard; gold locket with "R.A.B." note (fake Horcrux); Sneakoscope; Cedric Diggory badge; Daily Prophet; "Practical Defensive Magic and its Use Against the Dark Arts" (book Harry hides the obituary in); Transfiguration Today, Challenges in Charming, The Practical Potioneer (journals Dumbledore published in).

**Numbers/specifics:** Dumbledore discovered twelve uses of dragon's blood. The famous Dumbledore-Grindelwald duel took place in 1945. Skeeter's biography is nine hundred pages long, written in four weeks after Dumbledore's death in June.

**Obscure details:** Harry recalls the only personal question he ever asked Dumbledore: what Dumbledore sees in the Mirror of Erised — Dumbledore claimed to see himself holding a pair of thick, woollen socks (likely a lie). Harry throws the newspaper onto his rubbish pile after reading it.

## Chapter 3: The Dursleys Departing

**Plot events:**
- Vernon Dursley changes his mind yet again about going into hiding, accusing Harry's story of being a plot to steal the house.
- Harry argues, explaining the Trace, Death Eaters, and Dementors; mentions Kingsley Shacklebolt and Arthur Weasley's earlier visit (Arthur once demolished half the living room).
- Dudley surprises everyone by asking to go with the Order's protection; this persuades Vernon and Petunia to accept help.
- Dedalus Diggle (wearing a mauve top hat) and Hestia Jones arrive to escort the Dursleys to safety by car, then Portkey.
- Dudley tells Harry "I don't think you're a waste of space" and "You saved my life" (referencing the Dementor attack from book 5); shakes Harry's hand.
- Aunt Petunia almost says something to Harry before leaving but does not.
- The Dursleys depart by car.

**Named characters:** Vernon Dursley, Petunia Dursley, Dudley Dursley, Harry Potter, Dedalus Diggle, Hestia Jones, Kingsley Shacklebolt (mentioned, guarding the Muggle Prime Minister), Arthur Weasley (mentioned).

**Objects/places:** Number four, Privet Drive; Dedalus's pocket watch (which shouts "Hurry up!").

**Numbers/specifics:** Harry has lived at Privet Drive sixteen years ("Sixteen years of being addressed thus"). Protective charm breaks when Harry turns seventeen or no longer calls the house home.

**Obscure details:** Vernon wears a fawn zip-up jacket; Petunia a salmon-coloured coat; Dudley a leather jacket. Vernon had been packing/unpacking the car repeatedly for four weeks. Dudley's dumb-bells caused Vernon to collapse once while loading the car.

## Chapter 4: The Seven Potters

**Plot events:**
- Order members arrive at Privet Drive via Hagrid's flying motorbike (Sirius's old bike, with sidecar), brooms, and thestrals, using Disillusionment Charms.
- Present: Hagrid, Hermione, Ron, Fred and George Weasley, Bill Weasley, Arthur Weasley, Mad-Eye Moody, Tonks (reveals she has secretly married Lupin), Remus Lupin, Fleur Delacour, Kingsley Shacklebolt, Mundungus Fletcher.
- Moody explains: Pius Thicknesse (now Imperiused) has made Apparition/Portkey/Floo illegal at Privet Drive; Harry still has the Trace; the Order will use seven decoy "Harry Potters" via Polyjuice Potion, each paired with a protector, flying to different safe houses by broom/thestral/motorbike.
- Harry gives his hair reluctantly for the Polyjuice; Ron, Hermione, Fred, George, Fleur, and Mundungus transform into Harry doubles.
- Pairings: Moody & Mundungus (broom); Arthur & Fred... (confusion over Fred/George identity — corrected to George with Arthur initially mentioned then Lupin); Bill & Fleur (thestral); Kingsley & Hermione (thestral); Tonks & Ron (broom); Harry & Hagrid (motorbike, Harry in sidecar).
- They depart; are ambushed in the air by roughly thirty Death Eaters.
- Hedwig is killed by a Killing Curse while in her cage during the chaos.
- Harry and Hagrid are chased; Harry fights off several Death Eaters with Stunning Spells and the motorbike's gadgets (brick wall, net, dragon-fire from buttons, courtesy of Arthur Weasley's tinkering).
- The sidecar is damaged by Hagrid's clumsy "Reparo" with his pink umbrella and breaks away; Harry uses Wingardium Leviosa to keep it aloft, then blows it up with Confringo after pulling his rucksack free.
- Harry recognizes a Death Eater as the Imperiused Stan Shunpike (former Knight Bus conductor) and only Disarms him (Expelliarmus) rather than attacking — the enemy shouts "That's him, it's the real one!" revealing Harry's identity by his signature spell.
- Voldemort himself appears flying without a broom and pursues Harry and Hagrid; Harry's wand acts "of its own accord," producing gold flames and a "crack" that seems to repel Voldemort; Voldemort screams in fury and demands another Death Eater's wand (Selwyn's).
- Hagrid and Harry crash the motorbike into a pond near the Tonkses' house.

**Named characters:** Harry, Hermione Granger, Ron Weasley, Hagrid, Mad-Eye Moody, Fred and George Weasley, Bill Weasley, Arthur Weasley, Tonks, Remus Lupin, Fleur Delacour, Kingsley Shacklebolt, Mundungus Fletcher, Voldemort, Stan Shunpike, Selwyn (a Death Eater whose wand Voldemort demands), Hedwig (killed).

**Objects/spells/places:** Sirius's flying motorbike (with sidecar; secret buttons: brick wall, net, dragon-fire — Arthur Weasley's "tinkering"); Polyjuice Potion; Disillusionment Charm; the Trace; Firebolt (Harry's broomstick, lost); Stunning Spell (Stupefy), Impedimenta, Confringo, Wingardium Leviosa, Expelliarmus, Reparo (used wrongly by Hagrid with his umbrella).

**Numbers/specifics:** About thirty hooded Death Eaters ambush the group in a vast circle. Seven "Harry Potters" (the real one plus six decoys) with seven protectors/pairs fly to different safe houses (Moody names about a dozen possible safe houses, including his own, Kingsley's, and Molly's Auntie Muriel's).

**Obscure details:** Fred and George briefly confuse Moody about which twin is which while disguised as Harry. Fleur complains about how ugly she looks as "Harry." Ron notices as "Harry" that he (Harry) apparently has no tattoo, settling an earlier joke about Ginny lying about Harry having one. Hermione jokes about Harry's poor eyesight once she puts on his glasses.

## Chapter 5: Fallen Warrior

**Plot events:**
- Harry and Hagrid crash near the home of Ted and Andromeda Tonks (Tonks's parents); Ted patches up Harry's injuries (broken ribs, missing tooth regrown, arm).
- Harry learns the protective charms around the Tonkses' house repelled Voldemort (explaining why he vanished).
- They Portkey (an old silver hairbrush) to The Burrow.
- At The Burrow, Molly Weasley and Ginny rush out; news trickles in about the other pairs. Ron and Tonks' Portkey (a rusty oil can) arrives empty at first; George and Lupin arrive — George has lost an ear, cursed off by Snape using Sectumsempra.
- Lupin suspects treachery and tests Harry's identity by asking about the Grindylow in his office (confirming Harry is real), since Polyjuice couldn't account for it; explains Polyjuice doesn't work on half-giants (so Hagrid wasn't tested).
- Harry recounts seeing/disarming Stan Shunpike and that Voldemort chased him personally; Lupin scolds Harry for using "Expelliarmus" (his signature disarming spell) instead of more lethal force, calling it almost suicidal given Death Eaters now recognize the move.
- Hermione and Kingsley arrive safely (Kingsley injured/stunned several Death Eaters).
- Bill and Fleur arrive by thestral: they reveal Mad-Eye Moody is dead, killed by Voldemort's curse after Mundungus Fletcher panicked and Disapparated, abandoning him.
- Ron and Tonks arrive late, delayed when they missed their Portkey and had to go via Auntie Muriel's; Tonks reveals she fought Bellatrix Lestrange, who especially wants to kill her, and injured Rodolphus Lestrange.
- The group drinks a Firewhisky toast to Mad-Eye.
- Fleur voices suspicion that someone in the Order betrayed the date of the move (since the enemy knew the night but not the full plan); Harry defends the group's loyalty, refusing to suspect anyone, recalling his father, James, was betrayed by his friend Peter Pettigrew (Lupin draws the parallel).
- Bill and Lupin leave to try to recover Mad-Eye's body.
- Harry announces he wants to leave immediately to avoid endangering the Weasleys further; the others refuse to let him go.
- Harry's scar burns and he has a vision of Voldemort torturing wandmaker Ollivander in a dungeon, demanding to know why the "connection" between Harry's and Voldemort's wand defeated Lucius's borrowed wand; Ollivander insists the connection exists only between Voldemort's and Harry's own two wands.
- Hermione worries that Harry is letting Voldemort back into his mind and urges Occlumency.

**Named characters:** Harry, Hagrid, Ted Tonks, Andromeda Tonks ("Dromeda"), Molly Weasley, Ginny Weasley, Ron Weasley, Tonks (Nymphadora), George Weasley (loses an ear), Remus Lupin, Hermione, Kingsley Shacklebolt, Bill Weasley, Fleur Delacour, Mad-Eye Moody (confirmed dead), Mundungus Fletcher (fled, suspected of panicking not betrayal), Bellatrix Lestrange, Rodolphus Lestrange (injured), Fred Weasley, Arthur Weasley, Voldemort, Garrick Ollivander (tortured, held captive by Voldemort), Severus Snape (responsible for cursing off George's ear with Sectumsempra), Stan Shunpike, James Potter (Harry's father, mentioned), Peter Pettigrew (mentioned as betrayer of James).

**Objects/spells/places:** The Tonkses' house (protected by Order enchantments); silver hairbrush Portkey; The Burrow; Firewhisky; Sectumsempra (curse that cut off George's ear); Obliviate/memory-related topics not yet covered here; Stunning Spells; Gold flames from Harry's wand (unexplained wandlore).

**Numbers/specifics:** Twelve full glasses of Firewhisky poured for the toast to Mad-Eye (plus a thirteenth held by Bill) — thirteen people drinking to Mad-Eye. Lupin says the Trace breaks strictly at seventeen under wizarding law.

**Obscure details:** George jokes deliriously about being "holey" (holy) after losing his ear. Molly asks about Hedwig; Harry can't bring himself to say she's dead. Harry notices his own scar hadn't hurt like this "for more than a year." Lupin says James would have found it dishonourable to mistrust friends — foreshadowing Pettigrew's betrayal theme.

## Chapter 6: The Ghoul in Pyjamas

**Plot events:**
- Days after Mad-Eye's death, Harry is anxious to begin the Horcrux hunt but must wait until the Trace breaks at seventeen (in a few days) and after Bill and Fleur's wedding.
- Molly Weasley tries repeatedly to find out what Harry, Ron, and Hermione are planning (abandoning Hogwarts) but they refuse to tell her, citing Dumbledore's instructions.
- Molly keeps the trio apart doing wedding chores to delay their departure/plans.
- Ginny confronts Harry about his plan to "kill off Voldemort"; Harry evasively denies it, hurting her.
- The Burrow has become the new Order Headquarters since Grimmauld Place's secrecy (Fidelius Charm) is compromised — Dumbledore, the original Secret Keeper, died, making everyone he told a Secret Keeper, diluting the charm's strength among roughly twenty people.
- Harry worries Snape (who knows the Grimmauld Place address) could return there; Arthur mentions curses set to bind Snape's tongue if he tries.
- Ministry (under Scrimgeour) has not held a hearing on Harry's underage magic use, suspected to avoid admitting Voldemort's return and the Azkaban breakout.
- The trio finally discuss plans alone in Ron's attic room (with the family ghoul living in the crawlspace/attic above).
- Hermione reveals she modified her parents' memories, making them believe they are "Wendell and Monica Wilkins" who want to emigrate to Australia, to protect them from Voldemort — they don't know they have a daughter.
- Ron reveals the cover story: the family ghoul, disguised with fake pustules (via Fred and George) as "Ron" supposedly suffering from a contagious magical illness called spattergroit, will occupy Ron's bed to explain his absence from Hogwarts.
- Hermione reveals she used a Summoning Charm to retrieve several restricted books about Horcruxes from Dumbledore's office/library right after his funeral, including "Secrets of the Darkest Art," which explains explicitly how to make (and by extension destroy) a Horcrux.
- Discussion of Horcrux theory: only something extremely destructive beyond magical repair (like Basilisk venom) can destroy a Horcrux; phoenix tears are the only antidote to Basilisk venom; reforming a split soul from Horcruxes is possible only through genuine remorse, a process that can be fatally painful — unlikely for Voldemort to attempt.
- They discuss the fake locket's note signed "R.A.B." which claims the real Horcrux (the locket) will be destroyed.
- They debate whether to go to Godric's Hollow first (Harry's wish, to see his parents' graves and find answers) versus focusing on Horcruxes; Hermione worries Voldemort might be watching Godric's Hollow.
- The Delacour family (Fleur's parents Apolline and parents, plus sister Gabrielle, age eleven) arrives for the wedding.
- Harry turns seventeen; gets gifts: a gold watch from the Weasleys (previously belonging to Molly's brother Fabian, killed in the first war), a new Sneakoscope from Hermione, an enchanted razor from Bill and Fleur, chocolates from the Delacours, Weasleys' Wizard Wheezes merchandise from Fred and George.
- Minister Rufus Scrimgeour arrives uninvited at Harry's birthday dinner, delivering the contents of Dumbledore's will (held by the Ministry for 31 days under the "Decree for Justifiable Confiscation"): Ron receives Dumbledore's Deluminator; Hermione receives "The Tales of Beedle the Bard" (in runes); Harry receives the first Snitch he ever caught (in his first Quidditch match) — Scrimgeour explains Snitches have "flesh memories" and might open only for Harry since he never previously touched it bare-handed; Harry touches it in front of Scrimgeour — nothing happens then (it later responds privately with an inscription "I open at the close").
- Dumbledore had also left Harry the sword of Godric Gryffindor, but Scrimgeour refuses to hand it over, claiming it's a historical artifact not Dumbledore's to give, and questions Harry pointedly about why Dumbledore might want him to have a sword that could defeat the Heir of Slytherin — implying Harry is seen as "the Chosen One."
- Harry and Scrimgeour argue fiercely; Scrimgeour burns a small hole in Harry's shirt with his wand in anger, then storms off.
- That night, Harry and Ron examine the Deluminator (it can suck/release light) while Hermione reads the runic book; Harry, alone with the Snitch, kisses it, and the inscription "I open at the close" briefly appears before vanishing.
- (continues into Chapter 7/8 crossover)

**Named characters added:** Molly Weasley, Arthur Weasley, Ginny Weasley, Fred and George Weasley, Bill Weasley, Fleur Delacour, Apolline Delacour (Fleur's mother), Monsieur Delacour (Fleur's father), Gabrielle Delacour (age eleven, Fleur's sister), Rufus Scrimgeour (Minister for Magic), Albus Dumbledore (will), R.A.B. (mystery figure, referenced), Fabian Prewett (Molly's deceased brother, former watch owner), Pius Thicknesse (mentioned, Imperiused), Severus Snape (mentioned).

**Objects/spells/places:** The Deluminator (Ron's inheritance); The Tales of Beedle the Bard (Hermione's inheritance, written in runes); the first Snitch Harry ever caught (Harry's inheritance, inscribed "I open at the close"); the sword of Godric Gryffindor (withheld by Scrimgeour); Secrets of the Darkest Art (restricted Horcrux book); the family ghoul (fake "spattergroit" patient); Chudley Cannons (Ron's favorite Quidditch team, mentioned via posters); "Twelve Fail-Safe Ways to Charm Witches" (joke book gift from Ron, originally from Fred and George).

**Numbers/specifics:** The Ministry held the will's contents for 31 days under the Decree for Justifiable Confiscation. Harry turns seventeen. Molly's watch previously belonged to her brother Fabian (killed, implicitly, in the first Voldemort war). Hermione's parents are renamed "Wendell and Monica Wilkins," relocating to Australia.

**Obscure details:** Gregorovitch — Harry mutters this name in his sleep; Ron doesn't know who it is; Harry senses Voldemort is searching for this wandmaker abroad. The ghoul wears Ron's old pyjamas, has red hair and pustules to resemble Ron. The Weasley ghoul normally lives in the attic above Ron's room.

## Chapter 7: The Will of Albus Dumbledore

(Note: This chapter's content overlaps heavily with the birthday dinner and Scrimgeour's visit, which is detailed above under Chapter 6 as the chapter break occurs mid-scene in this text extraction. Key additional details:)

- Harry wakes from a dream muttering "Gregorovitch"; realizes Voldemort is searching for this foreign wandmaker, who made Viktor Krum's wand.
- Harry's seventeenth birthday begins: the Trace breaks, and he celebrates by using small bits of magic for fun (e.g., tying shoelaces, accio-ing his glasses, turning Ron's Chudley Cannons posters blue).
- Ginny gives Harry a private, lingering goodbye kiss in her bedroom (decorated with a Weird Sisters poster and a Gwenog Jones/Holyhead Harpies poster) before Ron interrupts; Ron is upset about Harry "messing Ginny around," and Harry promises not to continue the relationship, for Ginny's safety.
- Charlie Weasley arrives for the wedding (his best-man duties), forced into a haircut by Molly; it's revealed Hagrid's dragon "Norbert" (hatched in book 1) is actually female and now called "Norberta," cared for by Charlie in Romania.
- Scrimgeour's confrontation and the Will reading occurs as described above (merged with Ch.6 notes): Deluminator to Ron, Tales of Beedle the Bard to Hermione, the first Snitch to Harry, and the sword of Godric Gryffindor withheld.
- That night Harry, Ron, and Hermione puzzle over the bequests' meaning, with no success deciphering "I open at the close" or why Dumbledore wanted Harry to have the sword without simply giving it to him while alive.
- Ron reveals he actually knows the traditional stories in The Tales of Beedle the Bard (unlike Muggle-raised Harry and Hermione) — mentions titles: "The Fountain of Fair Fortune," "The Wizard and the Hopping Pot," "Babbitty Rabbitty and her Cackling Stump."

**Numbers/specifics:** Harry turns seventeen this chapter (birthday itself). Six years since Hagrid first told Harry he was a wizard (referenced "six years ter the day").

**Obscure details:** Dumbledore's watch-gift parallel (Harry gets a watch from the Weasleys like Ron got "for his seventeenth" the prior year). Krum's wand was made by Gregorovitch — foreshadowing the Elder Wand plot thread.

## Chapter 8: The Wedding

**Plot events:**
- Bill and Fleur's wedding takes place at The Burrow under a grand white marquee; Harry attends disguised via Polyjuice Potion as a red-headed Muggle boy from Ottery St Catchpole, introduced as "Cousin Barny."
- Harry, Ron, Fred, and George greet guests. Notable guests: Viktor Krum (invited by Fleur, as a family friend of the Delacours); Xenophilius Lovegood and his daughter Luna (both wearing bright robes; Xenophilius wears a strange triangular eye-like symbol as a pendant); Great Aunt Muriel (age 107, owns a goblin-made tiara lent to Fleur, is rude to everyone, mentions Fred and George once set off a Dungbomb under her chair).
- Elphias Doge attends; Harry speaks with him about Dumbledore and Rita Skeeter's claims.
- Auntie Muriel, drunk, reveals old family gossip to Harry: that Dumbledore's sister Ariana was rumored to be a Squib, was kept hidden/locked away by the family, never attended Hogwarts or was seen by a Healer at St Mungo's; debates whether Kendra Dumbledore or Ariana caused the other's death; reveals Aberforth punched Albus in the face at Ariana's funeral, blaming him for her death; claims her source is Bathilda Bagshot (author of "A History of Magic" and a Dumbledore family friend/neighbor) who likely also was Rita Skeeter's secret source. Muriel suggests Godric's Hollow (where Bathilda lives) is worth visiting for old photos/letters.
- Harry learns for the first time that the Dumbledores lived in Godric's Hollow (moving there after Percival's imprisonment) and that Bathilda Bagshot was their neighbor — devastated Dumbledore never mentioned this shared connection.
- Viktor Krum recognizes the symbol on Xenophilius's robes as Grindelwald's mark, carved by Grindelwald at Durmstrang; Krum says Grindelwald killed his grandfather.
- Mid-celebration, a silvery lynx Patronus (Kingsley Shacklebolt's) bursts in with the message: "The Ministry has fallen. Scrimgeour is dead. They are coming."
- Panic ensues; Death Eaters begin arriving at the wedding.

**Named characters:** Harry (as "Barny"), Ron, Hermione, Fred, George, Bill Weasley, Fleur Delacour, Molly Weasley, Arthur Weasley, Charlie Weasley, Percy Weasley (mentioned as absent — estranged), Xenophilius Lovegood, Luna Lovegood, Viktor Krum, Auntie Muriel, Elphias Doge, Bathilda Bagshot (mentioned), Rita Skeeter (mentioned), Monsieur and Madame Delacour, Gabrielle Delacour, Ginny Weasley, Lee Jordan, Perkins (Arthur's old Ministry colleague), Hagrid, Tonks, Remus Lupin, Rufus Scrimgeour (dead, announced), Kingsley Shacklebolt (Patronus messenger), Gellert Grindelwald (mentioned), Fenrir Greyback (mentioned in passing re: Bill's scarring), Dolohov and Rowle (unnamed yet, appear next chapter), Stan Shunpike (mentioned).

**Objects/places/spells:** The wedding marquee (white, gold balloons, golden dance floor later); Polyjuice Potion (Harry's disguise, "Cousin Barny"); Tales of Beedle the Bard discussed further; the Deluminator used to extinguish/restore light; the Patronus Charm (Kingsley's silver lynx); Grindelwald's mark/symbol (a triangular eye-like rune) worn by Xenophilius.

**Numbers/specifics:** Auntie Muriel is 107 years old. The wedding is held the day after Harry's seventeenth birthday.

**Obscure details:** Uncle Bilius (Ron's late uncle) reportedly once saw a Grim and died 24 hours later, and used to drink a whole bottle of Firewhisky and pull flowers out of an unspecified place during parties. The wedding cake is topped with two model phoenixes that take flight when cut. George jokes are referenced; Fred flirts with French Veela cousins. The wedding officiant is the same small, tufty-haired wizard who presided at Dumbledore's funeral.

## Chapter 9: A Place to Hide

**Plot events:**
- Amid the chaos of the Ministry's fall, Harry, Ron, and Hermione Disapparate (Side-Along with Hermione) away from The Burrow to Tottenham Court Road, London (a Muggle area Hermione picked at random).
- Hermione reveals she packed a beaded bag with an Undetectable Extension Charm, containing camping supplies, books, clothes, and Harry's Invisibility Cloak — she had prepared for a quick escape for days.
- They hide in a side-street then a shabby all-night café; two Death Eaters disguised as Muggle workmen (identified as Antonin Dolohov and Thorfinn Rowle) attack them.
- A duel breaks out: Ron saves Hermione by shoving her aside; Harry (still under the Cloak) stuns Rowle; Dolohov is petrified by Hermione's "Petrificus Totalus"; a stray spell knocks out the waitress.
- They repair the café, obliviate the Death Eaters' and the waitress's memories of the event, and debate how the Death Eaters found them — fearing Harry might still have a Trace or some other tracking method on him.

## Chapter 21: The Tale of the Three Brothers

The second brother demands the power to recall the dead and is given a stone, but the spirit he recalls (a girl he had loved) remains sad and remote, driving him to suicide to truly join her. The third and wisest/humblest brother asks only for a means to escape Death's notice and is given Death's own Cloak of Invisibility; he lives a long, full life, eventually passes the Cloak to his son, and greets Death as an old friend when his time finally comes.

## Chapter 22: The Deathly Hallows

## Chapter 24: The Wandmaker

- The trio flies on the stolen, nearly-blind dragon for hours, unable to steer, crossing much of Britain; they eventually force it to descend toward a lake and jump off into the water before it can realize it's carrying passengers, swimming to shore while the dragon flies on.

- Harry suddenly receives an unusually clear, violent vision through his scar: Voldemort, informed by a terrified goblin that "the Potter boy" and accomplices broke into the Lestrange vault and stole a small golden cup, flies into a murderous rage, kills the goblin and several Death Eaters present (including implied others, though Bellatrix and Lucius escape by hiding behind others) with the Elder Wand, then obsessively worries whether Harry knows about his other Horcruxes.
- Through this vision, Harry learns Voldemort's exact mental checklist of Horcrux locations: the ring (hidden in the Gaunt shack), the locket (hidden in the cave, unaware it's already destroyed and replaced), the diary (already known destroyed), the cup (just stolen), Nagini the snake (to be kept close/protected personally from now on), and a Horcrux hidden somewhere at Hogwarts (location known only to Voldemort himself, tied to his "connection" to the castle) — confirming there is a seventh planned Horcrux-related object at Hogwarts. Voldemort resolves to check the Gaunt shack first (fearing Dumbledore might have traced his mother's family name) and to warn Snape that Harry might try to enter the school, without revealing why.
- Voldemort decides to bring Nagini with him from now on for her own protection, no longer sending her out alone.
- Harry shares this revelation with Ron and Hermione, convinced the final Horcrux is hidden at Hogwarts and that they must get there immediately before Voldemort checks on it himself; despite having no concrete plan, they resolve to travel to Hogsmeade and figure out a way into the castle.

**Named characters:** Harry, Ron, Hermione, Voldemort, an unnamed terrified goblin (killed), Bellatrix Lestrange and Lucius Malfoy (survive the massacre), Nagini, Severus Snape (to be warned), Dumbledore (referenced).

**Objects/places/spells:** The lake where they land and release the dragon; Hufflepuff's cup Horcrux (safely in Harry's pocket); the Elder Wand (used by Voldemort to kill multiple followers in rage); the Gaunt shack (ring Horcrux's original hiding place, to be checked first); the cave (locket Horcrux's empty hiding place, Voldemort unaware of the switch).

**Numbers/specifics:** None especially numeric.

**Obscure details:** Voldemort's panic reveals he did NOT feel the diary's destruction happen (rationalizing it as due to having "no body" at the time), which is why he doesn't immediately sense the locket's destruction either — an important plot-logic point about why Voldemort remains unaware his Horcruxes are being destroyed one by one.

## Chapter 28: The Missing Mirror

**Plot events:**
- The trio Apparates to Hogsmeade's High Street at night but immediately triggers a hidden alarm (a piercing scream, the "Caterwauling Charm") that alerts a dozen Death Eaters waiting inside the Three Broomsticks, confirming the village is a trap set specifically for Harry's expected return.
- Death Eaters attempt an Accio on the Invisibility Cloak (fails, confirming the Cloak's unique, unbeatable nature) and nearly catch them; the group debates releasing Dementors instead of killing Harry outright, since Voldemort wants to personally end Harry's life, not merely his soul.
- Trying to Disapparate fails (anti-Disapparition wards are in place); Dementors close in, and Harry is forced to cast a Patronus (his silver stag) to repel them, inadvertently revealing his location.
- A mysterious rescuer — the Hog's Head's gruff barman — pulls them inside his pub just in time and hides them upstairs; he cleverly convinces the Death Eaters outside that the Patronus they saw was actually his own (claiming his Patronus is a goat, producing a horned Patronus to back the lie), and that the alarm was triggered by him letting his cat out, defusing the confrontation.
- Upstairs, Harry recognizes the barman's piercing blue eyes and a small two-way mirror on the mantelpiece (twin to Harry's broken one) — revealing this man is Aberforth Dumbledore, Albus's estranged brother, who has been secretly watching over Harry via the mirror (bought from Mundungus Fletcher) on Dumbledore's prior instruction, and who sent Dobby to rescue them from Malfoy Manor's cellar.
- Only one painting decorates Aberforth's rooms: a portrait of his late sister, Ariana Dumbledore.
- Ron realizes Aberforth isn't the mysterious doe-Patronus caster either (his Patronus is confirmed a goat).
- Aberforth, bitter and protective, urges Harry to abandon Dumbledore's mission entirely, calling the Order of the Phoenix finished and the war lost, and bluntly questions whether Albus was ever honest with Harry or truly cared about him versus using him for "the greater good," paralleling Grindelwald's old ideology.
- Aberforth then recounts, for the first time in the book from his own perspective, the full tragic family history: his sister Ariana was attacked by three Muggle boys at age six after they saw her doing involuntary accidental magic; the trauma left her magically unstable and mentally fragile, prone to uncontrollable outbursts; their father Percival was imprisoned in Azkaban for attacking the boys in revenge (never revealing the real reason, to protect Ariana from being institutionalized at St Mungo's); their mother Kendra was accidentally killed by one of Ariana's magical outbursts when Ariana was fourteen; young Albus Dumbledore then became head of the family and brought in the brilliant but dangerous Gellert Grindelwald, who, together with Albus, began planning wizard domination over Muggles ("for the greater good") while neglecting Ariana's care; when Aberforth confronted them before returning to school, a three-way duel broke out between Aberforth, Albus, and Grindelwald, and Ariana was accidentally killed in the chaotic crossfire, with none of the three men sure who cast the fatal curse.
- Harry reveals a crucial detail never previously shared: that Dumbledore, while dying from the cave potion in book 6, had been hallucinating and begging an unseen tormentor not to hurt "them," offering himself instead — proof, Harry argues, that Dumbledore was never truly free of guilt over Ariana and Grindelwald, undermining Aberforth's bitter narrative that Albus moved on heartlessly.
- Aberforth challenges Harry to consider whether Dumbledore saw him too as expendable "for the greater good," just as Ariana had been; Harry, shaken, insists the war must be fought regardless and declares his unwavering intent to continue Dumbledore's mission to defeat Voldemort, even at the cost of his own life.
- Aberforth, relenting, reveals the secret passage behind Ariana's portrait (which depicts her walking down a painted tunnel) leading directly into Hogwarts; using the portrait as a messaging system, he summons someone through it — Neville Longbottom emerges, overjoyed to see Harry, Ron, and Hermione.

**Named characters:** Harry, Ron, Hermione, Aberforth Dumbledore (revealed as the Hog's Head barman and mirror's secret keeper), Ariana Dumbledore (portrait, backstory detailed), Percival Dumbledore, Kendra Dumbledore, Albus Dumbledore, Gellert Grindelwald, Neville Longbottom (arrives via the portrait passage), Mundungus Fletcher (sold Aberforth the mirror), Dobby (confirmed sent by Aberforth), Hagrid and Grawp (mentioned, hiding in a mountain cave since Hagrid's near-arrest), Elphias Doge (mentioned).

**Objects/places/spells:** The Hog's Head pub in Hogsmeade; the two-way mirror (twin to Sirius's broken one, bought by Aberforth); Ariana Dumbledore's portrait (conceals a secret tunnel to Hogwarts); the Caterwauling Charm (Hogsmeade's intruder alarm); Aberforth's goat Patronus; the Dementors stationed around Hogsmeade/Hogwarts.

**Numbers/specifics:** Ariana was attacked by Muggle boys at age six; Kendra died when Ariana was fourteen.

**Obscure details:** This chapter is the emotional/thematic core clarifying the Rita Skeeter revelations from Chapter 18 from a sympathetic insider's perspective — Aberforth confirms Ariana was not a Squib but a magically unstable "Obscurial"-like victim of trauma (never using that specific term in this book, but matching that concept). Aberforth's single decorated portrait (only Ariana, none of Albus) signals his enduring grief and estrangement from his brother.

## Chapter 29: The Lost Diadem

**Plot events:**
- Neville Longbottom, battered and scarred from defying the Carrows' disciplinary regime, joyfully leads Harry, Ron, and Hermione through the secret tunnel from the Hog's Head directly into the Room of Requirement at Hogwarts.
- Neville explains the dire state of Hogwarts under Headmaster Snape and the Death Eater siblings Alecto and Amycus Carrow: Amycus teaches a corrupted "Dark Arts" class requiring students to practice the Cruciatus Curse on those given detentions (Neville earned a facial scar refusing to comply); Alecto teaches propagandistic Muggle Studies justifying the new pure-blood order (Neville earned another scar for a defiant question about the Carrows' own blood status).

- The gathered students, having followed news of Harry's exploits via Potterwatch, excitedly confirm the Gringotts break-in and dragon escape have become legendary, celebrated even as rumors spread through the school (one student, Terry Boot, was punished by Carrow for discussing it openly).

**Named characters:** Harry, Ron, Hermione, Neville Longbottom, Aberforth Dumbledore, Severus Snape (Headmaster), Alecto and Amycus Carrow, Augusta Longbottom (Neville's grandmother, fights off captors), Dawlish (Auror, hospitalized by Augusta), Luna Lovegood (kidnapped, referenced), Ginny Weasley (stopped returning to school after Easter, referenced), Michael Corner (tortured for freeing a first-year), Seamus Finnigan, Lavender Brown, the Patil twins (Parvati/Padma), Terry Boot, Ernie Macmillan, Anthony Goldstein, Crabbe and Goyle (mentioned, enjoy practicing Cruciatus), Xenophilius Lovegood (referenced).

**Numbers/specifics:** Students have been hiding in the Room of Requirement for nearly two weeks. More than twenty students are present in the room when Harry arrives.

**Obscure details:** The Room of Requirement's growth is explicitly tied to Neville's precise, repeated requests (only excluding Carrow supporters), reinforcing the "ask for exactly what you need" rule of the Room introduced in book 5. Ron notes humorously that the Room's inability to produce food matches Hermione's Gamp's Law explanation from much earlier in their journey — a continuity callback.

- Harry's scar reveals Voldemort has just discovered, at the Gaunt shack, that the ring Horcrux is gone (destroyed by Dumbledore in book 6) — confirming time is desperately short.
- More DA members arrive via the fake Galleon summons, including Ginny Weasley, Fred and George Weasley, Lee Jordan, and Cho Chang; Harry reluctantly agrees to let the assembled students help search for the hidden object, without revealing it is a Horcrux, framing it only as something that can help defeat Voldemort and connected to Ravenclaw.

**Named characters:** Harry, Ron, Hermione, Neville Longbottom, Seamus Finnigan, Lavender Brown, Patil twins, Terry Boot, Ernie Macmillan, Anthony Goldstein, Michael Corner, Luna Lovegood, Dean Thomas, Ginny Weasley, Fred and George Weasley, Lee Jordan, Cho Chang, Rowena Ravenclaw (statue), Alecto Carrow, Voldemort (vision of Gaunt shack).

## Chapter 30: The Sacking of Severus Snape

**Plot events:**
- Alecto Carrow summons Voldemort via her Dark Mark upon spotting Harry; Luna Stuns her before any further action, but the noise wakes the Ravenclaw dormitories.
- Harry senses through his scar that Voldemort is now at the sea cave, about to discover the locket Horcrux has been replaced with R.A.B.'s fake (not yet realized, but close).
- Amycus Carrow arrives, furious and panicked about his sister being unreachable, fearing Voldemort's wrath for a false alarm; Professor McGonagall intervenes, orders the common room opened (correctly answering the eagle's riddle), and confronts Amycus, who crudely threatens her and spits in her face.
- Harry, enraged, steps out of the Cloak and casts the Cruciatus Curse on Amycus (echoing Bellatrix's long-ago taunt that one must truly "mean" an Unforgivable Curse to cast it effectively) — successfully incapacitating him, a morally troubling moment where Harry uses a Dark/Unforgivable curse himself.

## Chapter 31: The Battle of Hogwarts

**Plot events:**
- In the Great Hall, Professor McGonagall organizes a full evacuation of underage students via Filch and Madam Pomfrey, while allowing of-age students to choose to stay and fight; Ernie Macmillan and others volunteer to stay.
- Voldemort's amplified voice booms throughout the castle, offering a truce: hand over Harry Potter by midnight, and the school will be spared — an ultimatum met with silence, until Slytherin student Pansy Parkinson demands Harry be handed over; in a powerful show of solidarity, Gryffindor, Hufflepuff, and Ravenclaw students stand facing the Slytherins (not Harry), prompting McGonagall to have the entire Slytherin house escorted out for evacuation (a implicit vote of no confidence, not necessarily malice).
- Kingsley Shacklebolt outlines the battle plan: teachers will position fighters atop the three tallest towers (Ravenclaw, Astronomy, Gryffindor) for height advantage in spellcasting; Lupin and Arthur Weasley will lead fighters into the grounds; Fred and George volunteer to help defend secret passage entrances.
- With only minutes before the midnight deadline and no sign of Ron and Hermione, Harry searches alone, eventually consulting the ghost Nearly Headless Nick, who directs him to the Grey Lady — the ghost of Ravenclaw Tower, previously unknown to be the daughter of Rowena Ravenclaw herself: Helena Ravenclaw.
- Harry presses Helena for information about the lost diadem; she initially refuses, but relents and reveals her own tragic history: she stole her mother's diadem out of jealousy/ambition and fled to a forest in Albania; her dying mother sent a devoted suitor, the Bloody Baron, to retrieve her; when Helena refused to return, the enraged Baron stabbed her to death, then killed himself in remorse (explaining the Bloody Baron's chains and perpetual penance as a ghost). The diadem remained hidden inside a hollow tree in that Albanian forest.
- Helena admits she once told this secret to another student who flattered and charmed her into confiding — Harry immediately recognizes this as young Tom Riddle, deducing Voldemort retrieved the diadem from Albania (likely right after leaving Hogwarts, explaining his later choice of Albania as a hiding place during his wilderness years) and later smuggled it back into Hogwarts and hid it there the night he unsuccessfully applied to Dumbledore for a teaching position (paralleling the Chamber of Secrets backstory from book 6) — but its exact hiding place within the castle remains unknown.
- As midnight approaches, Harry is interrupted when Hagrid crashes through a window, having been thrown or having crash-landed with his boarhound Fang, signaling the battle has begun in earnest.

**Named characters:** Harry, Ernie Macmillan, Pansy Parkinson, Minerva McGonagall, Kingsley Shacklebolt, Remus Lupin, Arthur Weasley, Fred and George Weasley, Nearly Headless Nick, the Grey Lady/Helena Ravenclaw (revealed identity), Rowena Ravenclaw (her mother), the Bloody Baron (revealed backstory — killed Helena, then himself), Tom Riddle/Voldemort, Hagrid, Fang, Voldemort (ultimatum voice), Zacharias Smith (minor mention, pushing past first-years).

**Objects/places/spells:** The Great Hall (muster/evacuation point); Voldemort's amplified ultimatum voice; the lost diadem of Ravenclaw (location mystery partially solved: hidden somewhere in Hogwarts since the night young Riddle sought a teaching post); the Albanian forest hiding spot (historical, no longer relevant); the three towers (Ravenclaw, Astronomy, Gryffindor) used as battle positions.

**Numbers/specifics:** Voldemort's ultimatum deadline is midnight. The battle plan involves three towers and ground forces under Lupin and Arthur Weasley.

**Obscure details:** The Bloody Baron's chains, a recurring minor detail from earlier books (notably his relationship/intimidation of Peeves), are given full tragic backstory here for the first time. Voldemort hid the diadem in Hogwarts the same night he asked Dumbledore for the Defence/teaching job (established in book 6's memories) — the same visit during which he likely scouted for the Chamber of Secrets and other secrets, tying Horcrux lore together.

- Harry realizes the diadem is hidden in the Room of Requirement's "Room of Hidden Things," the same place he once stashed his old Potions textbook — deducing Voldemort believed (wrongly) that only he had ever discovered this secret room.
- Reunited with Ron and Hermione (who reveal they snuck into the Chamber of Secrets using Ron's newly-learned Parseltongue hiss, retrieved Basilisk fangs, and used one to destroy the cup Horcrux themselves), the trio returns to the Room of Requirement to check on Ginny, Tonks, and Augusta Longbottom (Neville's grandmother), all of whom insist on joining the fight rather than stay safely hidden.
- They reenter the Room of Hidden Things and locate the diadem (perched on a stone bust wearing a wig, the same bust Harry once used to hide his old textbook) but are ambushed by Draco Malfoy, Crabbe, and Goyle, who hope to capture Harry to win Voldemort's favor.
- Crabbe recklessly casts Fiendfyre (cursed, sentient fire) which spirals out of control, destroying much of the Room's hidden contents and nearly killing everyone present, including Crabbe himself, who dies in the flames; Harry, Ron, Hermione, and (reluctantly, at Malfoy's/Ron's insistence) Draco Malfoy and the unconscious Goyle escape on broomsticks, with Harry snatching the diadem from midair amid the inferno just before escaping. The diadem (confirmed as the final known Horcrux) is destroyed by the Fiendfyre itself.
- Fighting resumes in the corridors; Percy Weasley reunites with the group, dramatically defeating the Imperiused-puppet Minister Pius Thicknesse and announcing his resignation from the Ministry on the spot, reconciling fully with his family.
- Catastrophe strikes: an explosion caused by combined curses kills Fred Weasley instantly, found by Harry, Ron, Hermione, and Percy amid rubble — a devastating, sudden death witnessed directly by his brothers.
- Despite their grief, Harry, Ron, and Hermione press on (per Hermione's insistence that they alone can end the war) to hunt down Nagini, since Voldemort now keeps the snake magically protected and close by at all times.
- Amid escalating battle chaos (giant spiders from the Forbidden Forest, Grawp fighting a larger invading giant, Dementors repelled by multiple student/adult Patronuses including a poignant assist from Luna, Ernie, and Seamus), Harry witnesses Hagrid being swarmed and apparently carried off by giant spiders while trying to protect them.
- The trio flees toward the Whomping Willow, using the tree's paralyzing knot (Ron levitates a twig to trigger it, recalling Crookshanks's role in book 3) to access the tunnel to the Shrieking Shack, where Harry's visions indicate Voldemort has been waiting the entire battle, believing Harry will come to him rather than fight directly.
- Inside the Shrieking Shack (observed secretly via the tunnel and Invisibility Cloak), Harry witnesses Voldemort interrogate Severus Snape about why the Elder Wand still fails to perform at its legendary full power despite being taken from Dumbledore's tomb.
- Voldemort deduces the crucial wandlore truth: the Elder Wand's true allegiance never transferred to him because he did not personally defeat/kill its true previous master — and concludes (incorrectly, as later clarified) that Snape, who killed Dumbledore, must be the Elder Wand's true master as long as he lives.
- Believing he must kill Snape to claim the wand's full power, Voldemort sets his magically caged snake Nagini upon Snape, commanding her in Parseltongue to kill; Snape is fatally bitten in the neck and collapses as Voldemort departs coldly, taking Nagini (still enclosed in her protective floating cage) with him.
- Harry emerges from hiding and kneels by the dying Snape, who — in his final act — uses his last strength to pull Harry close and non-verbally offer his memories (a silvery substance pouring from his eyes, ears, and mouth), which Hermione conjures a flask to collect; Snape's final words are a plea for Harry to "look at" him, and he dies as Harry's green eyes meet his black ones.
- Voldemort's amplified voice then addresses the entire battlefield, praising the defenders' bravery but demanding surrender, ordering a temporary one-hour ceasefire to tend the dead and wounded, and issuing a direct ultimatum to Harry: come to the Forbidden Forest alone within the hour, or Voldemort will resume the attack and kill everyone, specifically naming the consequence should Harry hide instead of confronting him.

**Objects/places/spells:** The Room of Hidden Things (within the Room of Requirement); Fiendfyre (cursed sentient fire, one of the few things that can destroy a Horcrux, used recklessly by Crabbe); the diadem of Ravenclaw (final confirmed Horcrux, destroyed in the Fiendfyre); the Whomping Willow and the tunnel to the Shrieking Shack; the Elder Wand (Voldemort's wandlore realization: true allegiance passed to whoever defeated its previous master, i.e., Snape, who killed Dumbledore — not yet realizing it may actually belong to Draco or Harry per the "disarming" rule); Nagini's magical floating protective cage/sphere; Snape's memories (collected in a flask by Hermione).

**Numbers/specifics:** Voldemort gives a one-hour ultimatum/ceasefire at the chapter's end.

**Obscure details:** Ron and Hermione destroyed the cup Horcrux independently using a Basilisk fang retrieved from the Chamber of Secrets, with Ron successfully speaking Parseltongue (mimicking Harry) to open the Chamber for the first time in the series by someone other than Harry. Percy's "Did I mention I'm resigning?" line while defeating Thicknesse is one of the book's notable comic-relief-amid-tragedy beats. ## Chapter 33: The Prince's Tale

**Note:** This chapter is almost entirely composed of Harry viewing Snape's collected memories in the Pensieve (back in the castle, though the chapter doesn't specify exactly when he views them — implied immediately after, within the one-hour ceasefire). The content reveals Snape's entire life story and true allegiance. Given this chapter is memory-heavy and emotionally dense, below is a thorough paraphrased summary of the revealed backstory:

**Plot events (Snape's memories, viewed by Harry):**
- Young Severus Snape, a poor, neglected boy living near the Evans family in Cokeworth, is shown secretly watching and later befriending young Lily Evans, revealing to her (before either attended Hogwarts) that she is a witch, while his own home life (overheard arguing parents, a Muggle father) is implied to be unhappy; Snape's mother, Eileen Prince, was a witch, explaining his mixed heritage and his later self-styled nickname "the Half-Blood Prince."
- Snape and Lily become close childhood friends despite Lily's sister Petunia's jealousy and resentment (Petunia, wishing she too were magical, grows to despise both magic and Snape).
- At Hogwarts, Snape is Sorted into Slytherin while Lily goes to Gryffindor; their friendship continues for years despite house rivalry, but strains under the influence of James Potter and Sirius Black's cruel bullying of Snape, and Snape's own increasing involvement with a Slytherin crowd interested in the Dark Arts (including future Death Eaters).
- A pivotal memory (previously seen in part in book 5) is expanded: after a humiliating public bullying incident by James Potter (in front of Lily, who intervenes to help Snape), a furious, humiliated Snape calls Lily a "Mudblood" in front of classmates, permanently damaging their friendship despite his later remorse and repeated apologies.

Snape is horrified, accusing Dumbledore of "raising him like a pig for slaughter," but ultimately commits to the plan out of loyalty to Lily's memory, famously affirming to Dumbledore that his Patronus — a silver doe, matching Lily's own — has "always" been the same, confirming Snape was the one who sent the silver doe Patronus that led Harry to the sword of Gryffindor in Chapter 19, and that Snape has secretly aided Harry's cause throughout the book despite appearing to be a villainous Death Eater Headmaster.
- Additional memories clarify: Dumbledore orchestrated his own death at the top of the Astronomy Tower (book 6) by asking/ordering Snape to kill him, both to spare Draco Malfoy from having to commit murder (and the soul-destroying consequences) and to cement Snape's cover as a trusted Death Eater in Voldemort's eyes, ensuring Snape's position as Hogwarts Headmaster to protect students as best he could from within.
- The memories confirm Snape has been protecting students where possible under the Carrows' regime, feeding information to the Order of the Phoenix, and trying to safeguard Harry throughout the year, all while maintaining his necessary cover as a seemingly loyal Death Eater and harsh Headmaster.
- Overwhelmed, Harry emerges from the Pensieve with a final, crucial realization: because he is an unintentional Horcrux, he must allow Voldemort to kill him in order for Voldemort's soul-fragment within him to be destroyed, paving the way for Voldemort's ultimate defeat — accepting this, Harry prepares to walk into the Forbidden Forest to face Voldemort and die without resistance.

## Chapter 34: The Forest Again

**Plot events:**
- Alone in the empty Headmaster's office (all portraits have vacated their frames to observe the battle elsewhere in the castle), Harry absorbs the full truth: Dumbledore always intended for him to die, since Harry is an unintentional Horcrux and only Voldemort's own hand can destroy that soul-fragment.
- Harry resolves to walk to his death without resistance, reasoning that his own death is the final, necessary step to defeat Voldemort permanently (the sixth Horcrux, Nagini, would still need to be destroyed afterward — he privately hopes Ron and Hermione will finish that task).
- He checks his birthday watch; roughly half of Voldemort's one-hour deadline remains.
- Walking through the castle under the Invisibility Cloak, Harry passes Neville Longbottom and Oliver Wood carrying the body of Colin Creevey, a young underage student killed despite being told to evacuate; he glimpses the Great Hall's mourners without approaching anyone he loves, deliberately avoiding a goodbye.
- He deliberately confides a partial plan to Neville (without revealing he intends to die): that Nagini must be destroyed, instructing Neville to kill the snake if Ron and Hermione are unable to, ensuring a third person still knows the final Horcrux's necessity in case Harry does not survive.
- He passes Ginny comforting an injured, frightened student without revealing himself, aching with the urge to speak to her but resisting.
- Reaching the edge of the Forbidden Forest, Harry opens the Golden Snitch using the phrase "I am about to die," revealing the Resurrection Stone inside, cracked along the line representing the Elder Wand (confirming Dumbledore cracked it deliberately when he used it, as revealed in the next chapter).
- Turning the stone three times, Harry is joined by the resurrected "shadows" of his parents James and Lily, his godfather Sirius Black, and Remus Lupin — not true ghosts, but comforting, semi-corporeal echoes — who confirm dying is painless ("quicker and easier than falling asleep," per Sirius) and accompany him invisibly through the Forest, shielding him from the Dementors' despair.
- Guided by overheard Death Eaters Yaxley and Dolohov (returning from a failed patrol, revealing Voldemort's deadline has technically expired without Harry's surrender), Harry follows them to the clearing (the former lair of the giant spider Aragog) where Voldemort waits with his full assembled forces, including two giants, Bellatrix, the Malfoys, Fenrir Greyback, and a magically restrained, weeping Hagrid tied to a tree.
- Harry drops the Resurrection Stone (causing his parents and friends to vanish) and steps forward to confront Voldemort directly, declaring himself present; Voldemort, visibly startled that his bluff worked, casts the Killing Curse directly at Harry in front of the assembled Death Eaters, who roar in triumph as Harry falls.

**Named characters:** Harry, Neville Longbottom, Oliver Wood, Colin Creevey (dead, confirmed killed despite being underage), Ginny Weasley, James Potter, Lily Potter, Sirius Black, Remus Lupin (all as Resurrection Stone echoes), Yaxley, Antonin Dolohov, Voldemort, Bellatrix Lestrange, Lucius and Narcissa Malfoy, Fenrir Greyback, Thorfinn Rowle, Hagrid (bound/restrained), Fang (implied absent from this scene).

**Objects/places/spells:** The Resurrection Stone (used, final use in the book); the Golden Snitch (opened via the phrase "I am about to die" — the "close" referenced by "I open at the close"); the Invisibility Cloak; the Forbidden Forest (Aragog's former lair, site of Voldemort's camp); the Killing Curse (cast at Harry).

**Numbers/specifics:** Colin Creevey is confirmed dead, among the named casualties. Harry turns the Resurrection Stone three times to summon the four echoes.

**Obscure details:** Lupin's final words to Harry (via the Stone-echo) express hope that his newborn son Teddy will understand why he died, framing it as fighting for a better world for his child — an emotionally resonant moment given Lupin never meets his son. The chapter explicitly confirms Harry considers envying his parents' and Hedwig's quick deaths versus his own deliberate, "cold-blooded walk" to death, underscoring the unique courage required of him.

## Chapter 35: King's Cross

**Plot events:**
- After the Killing Curse strikes him, Harry finds himself in a strange afterlife-adjacent realm resembling a vast, pristine King's Cross train station, naked and unencumbered, with clothes appearing when he wishes for them.
- A small, flayed, whimpering, barely-human childlike creature lies suffering beneath a distant bench, implied to represent the maimed, unsalvageable remnant of Voldemort's own soul-fragment (the piece that had been living in Harry, now expelled and dying) — Dumbledore tells Harry nothing can be done to help it.
- Albus Dumbledore, restored and whole (his previously cursed hand healed, appearing youthful and content), meets Harry and confirms he is not dead; the Killing Curse destroyed the Horcrux-fragment of Voldemort's soul within Harry rather than killing Harry himself, because Voldemort unknowingly used Harry's own blood (taken in the graveyard ritual in Goblet of Fire) to rebuild his body — meaning Lily's old protective sacrifice/charm lives on inside Voldemort's own body, inadvertently anchoring Harry to life as long as Voldemort lives.
- Dumbledore explains the deeper wandlore mystery of Harry's original wand overpowering Voldemort's borrowed wand during the book's opening chase: the twin-core connection, combined with Voldemort unknowingly reinforcing the bond between Harry and himself (via the blood ritual), caused Harry's wand to absorb a measure of Voldemort's own power, making it uniquely capable of defeating anything connected to Voldemort specifically (but ordinary otherwise, explaining why Hermione could still break it).
- Dumbledore delivers his final, full confession: his own selfish youthful ambitions with Gellert Grindelwald, his guilt over Ariana's death (never resolved which of the three — Dumbledore, Aberforth, or Grindelwald — cast the fatal curse during their fight), his years of self-imposed exile from power (refusing the Minister for Magic position repeatedly, believing himself unfit to be trusted with authority after his youthful lust for power), his long-delayed duel with Grindelwald (feared not for personal safety but for the truth it might reveal about Ariana's death), and his eventual victory and capture of the Elder Wand.
- Dumbledore confirms the Peverell brothers were real, gifted wizards (not literal meetings with personified Death) who created the three Hallows; Ignotus Peverell is Harry's ancestor via the Potter family line, explaining why Dumbledore borrowed James's Cloak after Harry's birth (to examine it as a possible genuine Hallow) and inadvertently ended up in possession of two Hallows (Cloak and, later, the ring/Resurrection Stone) after James's death.
- Dumbledore admits he foolishly put on the ring Horcrux-Hallow out of desperate longing to see his dead family again, triggering its curse, which Snape then contained in his hand; this directly led to Dumbledore's own death timeline and his plan with Snape.
- Dumbledore clarifies that he deliberately withheld the full truth about the Hallows from Harry while alive, fearing Harry's "hot head" might seize them recklessly as Dumbledore once had; he affirms Harry is the true, worthy master of all three Hallows (Cloak inherited rightfully, Stone used for selfless comfort not resurrection-obsession, and implicitly the Wand through the unintended wandlore mechanics) because Harry never sought them for personal gain and ultimately chose to accept death rather than evade it.
- Dumbledore confirms Voldemort never understood or valued the Deathly Hallows as a complete concept, only coveting the Elder Wand as a generic "most powerful wand," and that Voldemort's ignorance of love, loyalty, and self-sacrifice is the core blind spot that will doom him.
- Harry is given a real choice: he can "board a train" and move on to death, or choose to return to life; Dumbledore cannot promise success but confirms Harry has "less to fear" from returning than Voldemort does, and that returning could prevent further deaths and soul-destruction. Harry chooses to return.

**Named characters:** Harry, Albus Dumbledore, the maimed Voldemort soul-fragment (unnamed, non-sentient), Voldemort (discussed), Gellert Grindelwald (discussed), Ariana, Kendra, Percival, and Aberforth Dumbledore (discussed), the Peverell brothers (confirmed as real historical wizards), James and Lily Potter, Ignotus Peverell.

**Objects/places/spells:** The afterlife "King's Cross" realm; the Resurrection Stone (now confirmed cracked along the Elder Wand's line specifically because Dumbledore used it, not randomly); the Invisibility Cloak (confirmed the genuine, perfect third Hallow, passed down the Peverell/Potter line); the Elder Wand (Dumbledore's capture of it from Grindelwald, and his reasoning about why he was "fit" only to wield it without boasting or killing).

**Numbers/specifics:** None especially numeric beyond established facts.

**Obscure details:** Dumbledore's closing line to Harry — affirming the experience is "happening inside your head" but asking why that should mean it isn't real — is one of the most quoted philosophical moments in the series, left deliberately ambiguous as to the literal metaphysical nature of the King's Cross scene.

## Chapter 36: The Flaw in the Plan

**Plot events:**
- Harry revives on the forest floor, playing dead; Voldemort, who also momentarily collapsed when his Killing Curse destroyed the Horcrux-fragment rather than Harry, anxiously demands confirmation of Harry's death.
- Narcissa Malfoy is sent to examine Harry's body; feeling his heartbeat and realizing he's alive, she quietly asks only whether her son Draco is alive and inside the castcastle — when Harry confirms yes, she deliberately lies to Voldemort and the assembled Death Eaters that Harry is dead, prioritizing her family's safety over Voldemort's cause.
- The Death Eaters celebrate; Voldemort has Harry's "body" subjected to a humiliating Cruciatus Curse display (which Harry, still playing dead, must endure feigning limpness, though the curse doesn't actually hurt him in this state) and then forces the devastated, weeping Hagrid to carry Harry's "corpse" back toward the castle as proof of victory, with Voldemort taunting the centaurs (who refused to fight) along the way.
- At the castle, Voldemort magically amplifies a triumphant, false proclamation that Harry died fleeing to save himself, offering the defenders amnesty if they surrender; the assembled survivors (Ron, Hermione, Ginny, McGonagall, and others) react with devastated disbelief and grief.
- Neville Longbottom defiantly refuses to join Voldemort despite flattery about his pure-blood status, declaring his continued allegiance to "Dumbledore's Army"; Voldemort, enraged, forces the Sorting Hat onto Neville's head and sets it ablaze as public punishment and to declare the abolition of Hogwarts' house system in favor of Slytherin supremacy.
- At this critical moment, reinforcements arrive: hundreds of Hogsmeade residents and former students' families storm the grounds; Grawp battles Voldemort's giants; centaurs (Bane, Ronan, Magorian) attack with arrows; Harry (reviving and using the Invisibility Cloak) casts a Shield Charm to protect Neville, who breaks free of his Body-Bind, pulls the sword of Gryffindor from the flaming Sorting Hat (a deliberate callback to the sword's "need and valour" retrieval mechanism), and beheads Nagini in a single stroke — destroying the final Horcrux.
- Total chaos erupts as the battle resumes inside the castle; house-elves led by Kreacher (wearing Regulus Black's locket) join the fight against the Death Eaters, invoking Regulus's memory.
- Amid the renewed battle, Molly Weasley duels and kills Bellatrix Lestrange in single combat after Bellatrix taunts her about Fred's death and threatens Ginny; Voldemort, enraged at Bellatrix's death, nearly kills Molly in retaliation, but Harry reveals himself by casting a Shield Charm (Protego) to block Voldemort's curse, dropping the Invisibility Cloak.
- Harry and Voldemort face off in the Great Hall in front of the entire assembled crowd (both armies, students, teachers, villagers); Harry reveals the full truth about Dumbledore's plan, Snape's true loyalty (and his doe Patronus, proving his lifelong love for Lily), and the crucial wandlore twist: because Draco Malfoy disarmed Dumbledore before Snape killed him, Draco (not Snape) became the Elder Wand's true master upon Dumbledore's death — and because Harry himself subsequently disarmed Draco at Malfoy Manor, the Elder Wand's true allegiance belongs to Harry, regardless of Voldemort's physical possession of it.
- Harry urges Voldemort to feel remorse as his last chance to repair his shattered soul; Voldemort refuses, casts the Killing Curse at Harry one final time while Harry casts Expelliarmus; the Elder Wand, true to its allegiance, refuses to kill its rightful master and instead rebounds Voldemort's own curse back at him, killing Voldemort instantly as the Elder Wand flies into Harry's waiting hand.
- The crowd erupts in celebration; Harry is swarmed by friends and allies. Kingsley Shacklebolt is named temporary Minister for Magic; Death Eaters are rounded up, Imperiused victims freed, and Azkaban prisoners released.
- Harry, Ron, and Hermione retreat to the Headmaster's office, where all the portraits (including a joyfully weeping Dumbledore) celebrate; Harry confirms he deliberately left the Resurrection Stone lost in the Forbidden Forest (choosing never to seek it again) but intends to keep the Invisibility Cloak permanently; he then repairs his original holly-and-phoenix wand using the Elder Wand's power, and declares his intention to return the Elder Wand to Dumbledore's tomb, reasoning that if he dies a natural death without ever being defeated in combat, the Elder Wand's deadly cycle of conquest will finally end.

**Named characters:** Harry, Voldemort (dies, final death), Narcissa Malfoy, Lucius Malfoy, Draco Malfoy, Bellatrix Lestrange (killed by Molly Weasley), Molly Weasley, Hagrid, Neville Longbottom, Nagini (killed, final Horcrux destroyed), Kreacher, Ginny Weasley, Ron Weasley, Hermione Granger, Minerva McGonagall, Kingsley Shacklebolt (named temporary Minister for Magic), the centaurs Bane, Ronan, and Magorian, Grawp, Buckbeak the Hippogriff, Fenrir Greyback, Walden Macnair, Antonin Dolohov, Yaxley, Pius Thicknesse, Horace Slughorn, Charlie Weasley, Percy Weasley, Arthur Weasley, George Weasley, Lee Jordan, Albus Dumbledore (portrait), Phineas Nigellus (portrait), Fabian Prewett (Harry's watch, mentioned), Garrick Ollivander (implied, wandlore confirmed).

**Objects/places/spells:** The Sorting Hat (burned by Voldemort, yields the sword of Gryffindor to Neville); the sword of Gryffindor (used to decapitate Nagini, the final Horcrux); the Elder Wand (true allegiance resolved: Draco disarmed Dumbledore, then Harry disarmed Draco, making Harry its true master; ultimately rebounds Voldemort's final curse); the Invisibility Cloak (kept permanently by Harry); the Resurrection Stone (deliberately lost/abandoned in the Forbidden Forest); Harry's repaired holly-and-phoenix wand (fixed using the Elder Wand's power via Reparo); Regulus Black's locket (worn by Kreacher during the house-elf charge).

## Epilogue: Nineteen Years Later

**Plot events:**
- Nineteen years after the Battle of Hogwarts, Harry, Ginny, and their three children — James Sirius, Albus Severus, and Lily Luna Potter — arrive at King's Cross Station's Platform Nine and Three-Quarters to send James (now starting his second year, teasing his younger brother) and Albus (starting his first year) off on the Hogwarts Express; young Lily is too young to attend yet and is upset about being left behind.
- Ron and Hermione are also there with their children, Rose (starting first year alongside Albus) and Hugo (younger, not yet school-age); Ron jokes about having Confunded his Muggle driving examiner to pass his driving test, to Hermione's mock exasperation.
- Draco Malfoy is briefly glimpsed on the platform with his wife (unnamed on-page, implied Astoria from later Rowling canon, not named in this book) and son Scorpius Malfoy, who is starting Hogwarts the same year; Draco exchanges only a curt, distant nod with Harry's group, suggesting lingering but muted tension between the families.
- James reports excitedly that he saw their older cousin Teddy Lupin (Remus and Tonks's son, now a young adult and Harry's godson) kissing Victoire Weasley (Bill and Fleur's eldest daughter) near the train, delighting the family with the prospect of them marrying and Teddy formally joining the family (he already regularly has dinner with Harry's family).
- Albus privately confesses his fear of being Sorted into Slytherin; Harry reassures him, revealing for the first time that the Sorting Hat considered placing him in Slytherin too, and that the Hat honors a student's personal choice/preference — comforting Albus by noting he was named "Albus Severus" after two Hogwarts headmasters, one of whom (Snape) was the bravest man Harry ever knew.
- As the train departs, Harry reflects that his lightning scar has not pained him in nineteen years, confirming Voldemort is permanently gone and "all was well."

# Cross-chapter facts (franchise-wide ledger material)

## The Seven Horcruxes — what, where, and how each was destroyed

Voldemort split his soul into (effectively) seven pieces by committing murder six times intentionally, housing six fragments in chosen objects/beings, with the seventh fragment remaining in his own body (destroyed with him at the end) and an accidental eighth fragment-split occurring when his first Killing Curse rebounded off baby Harry in 1981 — meaning there were, in total, six intentional Horcruxes plus Harry as an unintentional seventh, plus the soul-remnant in Voldemort's own body.

1. **Tom Riddle's diary** — destroyed in Book 2 (Chamber of Secrets) by Harry, stabbed with a Basilisk fang; referenced retrospectively in this book as the first Horcrux destroyed.
2. **Marvolo Gaunt's ring** (set with the Resurrection Stone, disguised as the "Peverell coat of arms") — hidden in the ruined Gaunt shack; destroyed by Dumbledore in late Book 6 using the sword of Gryffindor, but the ring's curse nearly killed him, blackening his hand; this book reveals (via Snape's memories, Ch.33, and Dumbledore's own account, Ch.35) that Dumbledore recklessly put the ring on, triggering the curse, contained temporarily by Snape. The stone inside survives (it's also a Hallow) and is later given to Harry inside the Snitch.
3. **Salazar Slytherin's locket** — originally hidden in the cave on the Horcrux island (guarded by Inferi and a basin of painful potion); stolen and replaced with a fake by Regulus Arcturus Black (R.A.B.), who had his house-elf Kreacher swap it and sacrificed himself to the Inferi (backstory revealed Ch.10). The real locket was later stolen from Kreacher by Mundungus Fletcher and given to Dolores Umbridge (Ch.11–13), who wore it claiming it was a "Selwyn" heirloom; Harry stuns Umbridge and takes it during the Ministry infiltration (Ch.13). It is finally destroyed in Ch.19 (The Silver Doe) by Ron Weasley, who stabs it with the sword of Gryffindor after Harry opens it via Parseltongue; the Horcrux fights back psychologically, taunting Ron about being least-loved/overshadowed, before being destroyed.
4. **Helga Hufflepuff's cup** — stolen from Hepzibah Smith (who died from poisoning, framed on her house-elf, back when Riddle worked at Borgin and Burkes); hidden in the Lestrange family vault at Gringotts. Retrieved by Harry, Ron, and Hermione (with Griphook's help and the dragon escape) in Ch.26 ("Gringotts"); destroyed in Ch.31 ("The Battle of Hogwarts") when Hermione stabs it with a Basilisk fang retrieved by Ron and Hermione from the Chamber of Secrets (Ron speaks Parseltongue to open the Chamber).
5. **Rowena Ravenclaw's diadem** — stolen by young Tom Riddle from Albania (location told to him by the ghost Helena Ravenclaw, the "Grey Lady," Ravenclaw's own daughter, who had stolen it from her mother and hidden it in a hollow tree before being murdered by the Bloody Baron, Ch.31); Riddle hid it inside Hogwarts' Room of Requirement (Room of Hidden Things) the night he sought the Defence/teaching post from Dumbledore. Located and destroyed in Ch.31 ("The Battle of Hogwarts") when it is consumed/destroyed in the Fiendfyre (cursed fire) accidentally unleashed by Vincent Crabbe, who dies in the same inferno; Harry snatches the diadem from the flames moments before its destruction is completed.
6. **Nagini** (Voldemort's snake) — kept physically close to Voldemort after he realizes his other Horcruxes are being hunted (from Ch.27 onward, magically protected in a floating enchanted cage/sphere). Decapitated by Neville Longbottom in Ch.36 ("The Flaw in the Plan") using the sword of Gryffindor, drawn from the burning Sorting Hat, during the final battle.
7. **Harry Potter himself** (accidental seventh Horcrux) — created when Voldemort's first Killing Curse rebounded in Godric's Hollow on 31 October 1981, splitting off a soul-fragment that latched onto baby Harry, the only living soul in the collapsing house. Revealed to Harry via Snape's memories (Ch.33) and confirmed by Dumbledore's portrait/afterlife explanation (Ch.35). "Destroyed" in Ch.34/36 when Voldemort casts the Killing Curse at Harry in the Forbidden Forest: the curse kills only the Horcrux-fragment of Voldemort's soul, not Harry himself, because Voldemort had unwittingly used Harry's own blood (taken in the graveyard ritual, Book 4) to rebuild his body, meaning Lily's old protective charm/sacrifice lived on inside Voldemort, inadvertently keeping Harry tethered to life as long as Voldemort lived.

(Plus the final soul-fragment remaining in Voldemort's own body/wraith, finally destroyed with his death at the end of Chapter 36.)

## The Three Deathly Hallows

Created by/associated with the legendary three Peverell brothers (Antioch, Cadmus, and Ignotus), as told in "The Tale of the Three Brothers" (read aloud by Hermione from The Tales of Beedle the Bard, Ch.21). Dumbledore confirms (Ch.35) the brothers were real, gifted wizards; the "meeting Death" framing is likely later legend built around their creations.

1. **The Elder Wand** (also called the Deathstick / Wand of Destiny) — the eldest brother Antioch's Hallow; an unbeatable wand whose true allegiance passes to whoever defeats (disarms, kills, or otherwise overpowers) its previous owner. Documented history includes owners such as Egbert the Egregious, Emeric the Evil, Godelot, his son Hereward, Loxias, Barnabas Deverill, Arcus, and Livius (per Xenophilius Lovegood, Ch.21); more recently passed from wandmaker Gregorovitch (stolen by young Gellert Grindelwald), to Grindelwald (defeated and disarmed by Dumbledore in their famous 1945 duel), to Dumbledore (who kept it quietly at Hogwarts without boasting of it), to — upon Dumbledore's death — Draco Malfoy (because Draco disarmed Dumbledore atop the Astronomy Tower in Book 6, even though Snape struck the fatal curse per Dumbledore's own arranged plan), to Harry (because Harry disarmed Draco at Malfoy Manor, Ch.23). Voldemort steals the wand from Dumbledore's tomb (Ch.24) but it never truly serves him since he never defeated its true master. In the final duel (Ch.36), Voldemort's own rebounding Killing Curse (cast via the Elder Wand against its true master) kills him instead, and the wand flies to Harry. Harry ultimately repairs his own original wand using the Elder Wand's power, then returns the Elder Wand to Dumbledore's tomb, intending it to lose its power when he eventually dies undefeated.
2. **The Resurrection Stone** — the second brother Cadmus's Hallow; can summon shade-like echoes of the dead, who appear sad and semi-real, not truly alive. Mistaken for generations as an ordinary ring with a "Peverell coat of arms" by the Gaunt family (Voldemort's maternal ancestors); turned into Voldemort's ring Horcrux without his realizing it was also a Hallow. Recovered by Dumbledore late in Book 6, who foolishly put it on, triggering its Horcrux curse (contained by Snape) — Dumbledore says he wanted to see his dead family and apologize to them. Dumbledore hid the stone inside the Golden Snitch bequeathed to Harry in his will (opens with the phrase "I open at the close"). Harry uses it once, in the Forbidden Forest (Ch.34), to summon the echoes of James and Lily Potter, Sirius Black, and Remus Lupin for comfort before his confrontation with Voldemort, then deliberately drops/abandons it in the Forest, never to be recovered.
3. **The Cloak of Invisibility** — the third, humblest brother Ignotus Peverell's Hallow; unlike ordinary invisibility cloaks/charms, it provides perfect, permanent, unbeatable concealment. Passed down through the Peverell line to the Potter family (Ignotus is buried in Godric's Hollow and is Harry's ancestor); this is the same cloak James Potter owned and lent to Dumbledore shortly before his death for examination (explaining a mysterious detail in Lily's letter, Ch.10), and which Dumbledore kept after James and Lily died, eventually returning it to teenage Harry in Book 1. Harry uses it throughout the book and keeps it permanently at the end (Ch.36) rather than returning it to Dumbledore's portrait's "collection."

A person possessing all three Hallows becomes, per legend, "Master of Death." Harry unites all three by the book's end (briefly) but chooses not to pursue unity/power deliberately, discarding the Stone and returning the Wand to the tomb, keeping only the Cloak as a family heirloom.

## Battle of Hogwarts — named casualties (confirmed dead)

- **Fred Weasley** — killed instantly by a collapsing wall/explosion during a lull in fighting, alongside Percy, Ron, Hermione, and Harry (Ch.31); the Weasley family is devastated, witnessed directly by his siblings.
- **Remus Lupin** — killed during the battle (death is discovered/shown only afterward, found laid out in the Great Hall beside Tonks and Fred, Ch.33); leaves behind his infant son, Teddy Lupin.
- **Nymphadora Tonks** — killed during the battle (same discovery scene); she had left her infant son Teddy with her mother, Andromeda, to fight.
- **Colin Creevey** — underage student who snuck back to fight despite evacuation orders; found dead and carried in by Neville and Oliver Wood (Ch.34).
- **Severus Snape** — killed by Voldemort's order (Nagini's bite, Ch.32) in the Shrieking Shack, for the Elder Wand's allegiance (a murder, not a battle casualty per se, but occurring during the night of the battle).
- **Bellatrix Lestrange** — killed by Molly Weasley in a duel after taunting her about Fred's death and threatening Ginny (Ch.36) — a Death Eater casualty, not a defender, but notable.
- **Vincent Crabbe** — dies in the Room of Requirement, killed by his own uncontrolled Fiendfyre (Ch.31) — a Death Eater/student-ally casualty.
- **Voldemort (Tom Riddle)** — dies at the very end (Ch.36), killed by his own rebounding Killing Curse after Harry's Expelliarmus and the Elder Wand's true allegiance.
- The text also references approximately fifty total dead laid out in a separate area from Voldemort's body (Ch.36), of which only Fred, Tonks, Lupin, and Colin Creevey are explicitly named; many others (implied additional students, Order members, and Death Eaters) remain unnamed in the text.
- Note: Mad-Eye Moody (killed in Ch.4/5, "Fallen Warrior," at the start of the book, not during the Hogwarts battle) and Dobby the house-elf (killed by Bellatrix's thrown knife at the end of Ch.23/opening Ch.24, also before the battle) are earlier book-7 deaths, not Hogwarts-battle casualties, but are major named deaths within this book overall.
- Hagrid, Hogwarts staff, and all main trio members (Harry, Ron, Hermione, Ginny, Neville, Luna) survive.

## Epilogue details ("Nineteen Years Later")

- Setting: Platform Nine and Three-Quarters, King's Cross, 1 September, nineteen years after the Battle of Hogwarts.
- Harry Potter and Ginny Weasley are married with three children: **James Sirius Potter** (starting second year), **Albus Severus Potter** (starting first year, named after Albus Dumbledore and Severus Snape), and **Lily Luna Potter** (too young for Hogwarts, implied around age 9).
- Ron Weasley and Hermione Granger are married with two children: **Rose Weasley** (starting first year with Albus) and **Hugo Weasley** (younger, not yet school-age).
- Draco Malfoy appears with his wife (unnamed in the text) and son **Scorpius Malfoy**, also starting first year; relations between the Potter/Weasley group and the Malfoys remain cool but civil (a curt nod exchanged).
- **Teddy Lupin** (Remus and Tonks's son, Harry's godson) is now a young adult, seen romantically involved with **Victoire Weasley** (Bill and Fleur's eldest daughter); he reportedly already dines with Harry's family regularly.
- Neville Longbottom is confirmed to now be a Hogwarts professor (implied Herbology), addressed by students as "Professor Longbottom."
- Albus confides his fear of being Sorted into Slytherin; Harry reveals for the first time that the Sorting Hat considered Slytherin for him too, and reassures Albus that the Hat honors personal choice, and that Severus Snape (one of his namesakes) was "probably the bravest man" Harry ever knew.
- Percy Weasley is mentioned (pompously discussing broomstick regulations), confirming his full reintegration into the family after his Chapter-31 reconciliation.
- Final line: Harry's scar has not hurt in nineteen years, confirming Voldemort's permanent defeat — "All was well."

## Resolution of the central antagonist (Voldemort)

Lord Voldemort (Tom Marvolo Riddle) is permanently destroyed at the climax of the Battle of Hogwarts (Chapter 36, "The Flaw in the Plan"). After all seven of his Horcruxes (diary, ring, locket, cup, diadem, Nagini, and the accidental fragment in Harry) are destroyed over the course of the book (the diary in an earlier book), Voldemort duels Harry Potter directly in the Great Hall in front of the assembled students, staff, Order of the Phoenix, Hogsmeade villagers, and his own Death Eaters. Voldemort, holding the Elder Wand (stolen from Dumbledore's tomb), is unaware that its true magical allegiance belongs to Harry (via the chain of disarming: Draco disarmed Dumbledore, then Harry disarmed Draco). When Voldemort casts the Killing Curse at Harry one final time while Harry casts the disarming charm Expelliarmus, the wand refuses to kill its true master and instead rebounds Voldemort's own curse back upon him, killing him instantly. His body collapses, "feeble and shrunken," and Harry catches the Elder Wand. Voldemort's reign ends; Kingsley Shacklebolt becomes (temporary, later presumably permanent per later canon) Minister for Magic, Death Eaters are rounded up, and the wizarding world begins rebuilding. The epilogue, set nineteen years later, confirms the victory is permanent and lasting.
