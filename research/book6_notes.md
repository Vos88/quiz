## Chapter 1: The Other Minister

**Plot events (in order):**
- The Muggle Prime Minister sits in his office late at night, stressed about recent disasters: the Brockdale Bridge collapse (killed people, bridge was fewer than ten years old, snapped in two sending a dozen cars into the river), two "nasty" murders, a freak hurricane in the West Country, and Junior Minister Herbert Chorley acting strangely (impersonating a duck in public).
- A portrait of an ugly little man in a long silver wig (who has hung in the office since the PM's first day in office and cannot be removed) announces Cornelius Fudge wants an urgent meeting.
- Fudge arrives via the fireplace (Floo), looking careworn, thinner, balder, grayer than before.
- Flashback: the PM recalls his first meeting with Fudge on his first night in office years ago — Fudge turned his teacup into a gerbil to prove he was a wizard; the PM later gave the gerbil to his niece.
- Flashback: three years ago Fudge arrived soaking wet after a dementor breakout at Azkaban, warning of escaped prisoner "Serious" (Sirius) Black.
- Flashback: Fudge once informed the PM about importing three foreign dragons and a sphinx for the Triwizard Tournament.
- Flashback: a "mass breakout" from Azkaban (referring to events of book 5).
- Present: Fudge reveals the bridge collapse, the hurricane, and the murders were all actually caused by Voldemort's Death Eaters and giants — not natural disasters. He confirms Voldemort ("He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named") is truly back and "is alive... has a body and is walking and talking and killing."
- Fudge clarifies Sirius Black was innocent all along and was murdered on Ministry premises (an inquiry pending).
- Fudge reveals Amelia Bones (Head of the Department of Magical Law Enforcement) was murdered, likely personally by Voldemort — found in a room locked from the inside, having put up a real fight.
- Emmeline Vance was also murdered nearby.
- Dementors have left Azkaban and joined Voldemort; they are "breeding," causing the mysterious mist/gloom over Britain.
- Fudge reveals he was sacked as Minister of Magic three days ago after the Wizarding community demanded his resignation for a fortnight.
- Rufus Scrimgeour, former Head of the Auror Office, arrives via Floo as the new Minister of Magic, described as lion-like with tawny hair, bushy eyebrows, yellowish eyes, wire-rimmed spectacles, a slight limp.
- Scrimgeour locks the door and curtains the windows, insists on discussing the PM's security.
- Scrimgeour confirms Kingsley Shacklebolt (the PM's "secretary") is actually a highly trained Auror assigned to protect him.
- Scrimgeour explains Herbert Chorley was hit by a poorly performed Imperius Curse and must be removed to St. Mungo's Hospital for Magical Maladies and Injuries (he'd tried to strangle three Healers).
- Fudge will stay on in an "advisory capacity."
- The chapter ends with Scrimgeour's line that "the other side can do magic too."

**Named characters:** The Prime Minister (unnamed Muggle PM), Cornelius Fudge (former Minister of Magic), the portrait in the long silver wig (unnamed, announces visitors), Rufus Scrimgeour (new Minister of Magic), Lord Voldemort / He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named, Sirius Black ("Serious" Black, mispronounced by the PM), Herbert Chorley (Junior Minister, Imperiused, impersonates a duck), Amelia Bones (murdered, Head of Dept. of Magical Law Enforcement), Emmeline Vance (murdered), Kingsley Shacklebolt (Auror posing as PM's secretary), Dumbledore (mentioned — in correspondence with Fudge/Scrimgeour; newly reinstated Chief Warlock later mentioned next chapter), the PM's niece (received the gerbil), a carpenter/builder/art historian/Chancellor of the Exchequer (all failed to remove the portrait from the wall).

**Named places/objects/spells/creatures:** Azkaban prison (Middle of the North Sea), Ministry of Magic, Department of Magical Law Enforcement, Muggle Liaison Office, Office of Misinformation, Department for the Regulation and Control of Magical Creatures, St. Mungo's Hospital for Magical Maladies and Injuries, Brockdale Bridge, West Country (hurricane location), Hogwarts School of Witchcraft and Wizardry, Imperius Curse, dementors, giants, the Dark Mark, Metamorph-Medals (mentioned later, not this chapter).

**Specific numbers/details:**
- Bridge: fewer than ten years old, snapped in two, sent a dozen cars into the river.
- Fudge had "more than fifty eyewitnesses" pointing to Black's guilt (mistakenly).
- Scrimgeour was previously Head of the Auror Office.
- The mist has lasted since the middle of July (unseasonable chill).
- Voldemort vanished mysteriously fifteen years earlier (per PM's memory).
- Wizarding community had been "screaming for Fudge's resignation for a fortnight."

**Obscure/small details:** The portrait occupant is sometimes seen yawning, scratching his nose, or walking out of frame leaving muddy brown canvas. The PM trained himself to ignore it. Fudge's favored drink is whiskey; he conjures two glasses of amber liquid. Fudge once wrote Voldemort's name on parchment rather than say it aloud. The Prime Minister touches wood (knocks on his desk) superstitiously when thinking no murder has occurred in his departments.

## Chapter 2: Spinner's End

**Plot events (in order):**
- Two hooded women Apparate to a derelict Muggle mill town by a dirty river; one (Bellatrix) accidentally kills a fox with a flash of green light (Killing Curse), mistaking it for a possible Auror.
- The two are revealed as sisters: Narcissa Malfoy and Bellatrix Lestrange. Narcissa pursues on to a street called Spinner's End, dominated by an old mill chimney.
- They arrive at Severus Snape's house, the last house on the street. Snape lets them in.
- Inside: a small, book-lined sitting room. Peter Pettigrew ("Wormtail") is hiding in the house, his right hand encased in a silver magical hand (from book 4). Snape orders Wormtail to fetch elf-made wine then listen at doors — catches Wormtail eavesdropping and blasts the staircase door.
- Bellatrix interrogates Snape, questioning his loyalty: why wasn't he there when the Dark Lord fell; why didn't he search for Voldemort after his disappearance; why did he stay at Hogwarts; why did he block Voldemort/Quirrell from the Sorcerer's Stone; why did he take two hours to return when the Dark Mark burned; why is Harry Potter still alive after five years in Snape's power.
- Snape answers each: he was ordered to spy on Dumbledore at Hogwarts; he thought Voldemort was finished (like many other Death Eaters e.g. Avery, Yaxley, the Carrows, Greyback, Lucius); Dumbledore's protection kept him out of Azkaban; he gave Voldemort sixteen years of intelligence on Dumbledore upon his return; he blocked Quirrell (not realizing it was Voldemort) because Voldemort didn't trust him enough to reveal himself then; he waited two hours after the Dark Mark returned deliberately, on Dumbledore's orders, to preserve his position as a spy; he never killed Harry because that would've ended his usefulness/cover, and because Voldemort needed Harry's blood (for his resurrection in book 4) and because at first Snape was curious whether Harry might be a "standard" for Death Eaters to rally round.
- Snape also notes he refused the Defense Against the Dark Arts job because Dumbledore worried it would tempt him back to Dark Arts (confirming Snape has always wanted that subject).
- Snape reveals Dumbledore sustained a "serious injury" after a duel with Voldemort "last month" and that his reactions are slower.
- Narcissa begs Snape for help: her son Draco has been given a secret, dangerous task by Voldemort (unnamed in this chapter) as punishment/test related to Lucius's failure at the Ministry (end of book 5). Narcissa fears Voldemort intends Draco to die trying, and that Snape is meant to finish the job if Draco fails.
- Snape agrees to make an Unbreakable Vow with Narcissa, with Bellatrix as the Bonder (witness/binder), vowing to: (1) watch over Draco as he attempts to fulfill the Dark Lord's wishes, (2) protect him to the best of his ability, and (3) carry out the task himself if Draco should fail. Three fiery bonds wind around their clasped hands as each vow is spoken.

**Named characters:** Narcissa Malfoy ("Cissy"), Bellatrix Lestrange ("Bella"), Severus Snape, Peter Pettigrew ("Wormtail"), Lord Voldemort ("the Dark Lord"), Draco Malfoy (mentioned, not present), Lucius Malfoy (in Azkaban), Dumbledore, Harry Potter (discussed), Karkaroff (mentioned — "escape like Karkaroff"), Quirrell (mentioned), Amelia Bones/Emmeline Vance/Sirius Black (mentioned re: Order info), Avery, Yaxley, the Carrows, Fenrir Greyback (named as Death Eaters/followers who lost faith when Voldemort vanished).

**Named places/objects/spells/creatures:** Spinner's End (street), the disused mill and chimney, elf-made wine, the Unbreakable Vow, the Dark Mark, the Order of the Phoenix, the Ministry of Magic, the Sorcerer's Stone (Philosopher's Stone), Azkaban, Legilimens/Legilimency (Voldemort described as "most accomplished Legilimens").

**Specific numbers/details:**
- Snape says he gave Voldemort "sixteen years" of information on Dumbledore upon his return.
- Snape returned to Voldemort's side "two hours" after the Dark Mark burned, deliberately, on Dumbledore's orders.
- Harry has been "at Snape's mercy" for five years (i.e., since starting Hogwarts).
- Bellatrix says they "battled to retrieve the prophecy... a few weeks ago" (Ministry battle, book 5) against "six teenagers," later joined by "half the Order."
- Dumbledore's duel with Voldemort was "last month."

**Obscure/small details:** Snape's sitting room walls are covered in old black/brown leather-bound books; a single candle-lit lamp; the house has a neglected, rarely-inhabited feel. Snape keeps Wormtail essentially as an unwilling servant/lookout placed by Voldemort to "assist" him. Bellatrix is described as dark-haired with heavily lidded eyes and a strong jaw, in contrast to fair Narcissa. Narcissa's hair is described as long and blonde, giving her the look of "a drowned person" when wet with grief/pale skin.

## Chapter 3: Will and Won't

**Plot events (in order):**
- Harry waits anxiously in his room at Privet Drive for Dumbledore, having received a letter asking to collect him Friday at 11 PM. Newspaper clippings around his room show headlines: "HARRY POTTER: THE CHOSEN ONE?" about the Hall of Prophecy disturbance, and "SCRIMGEOUR SUCCEEDS FUDGE," plus a story headlined "MINISTRY GUARANTEES STUDENTS' SAFETY" quoting Augusta Longbottom praising her grandson Neville.
- A Ministry security pamphlet lists safety guidelines against Death Eaters (mentions Side-Along-Apparition, Polyjuice Potion, Imperius Curse, Inferi).
- Dumbledore arrives exactly at 11 PM, greets the stunned Dursleys (Vernon, Petunia, Dudley), forces them (via wandless manipulation of furniture) to sit and listen, and conjures Madam Rosmerta's oak-matured mead for everyone.
- Dumbledore tells Harry that Sirius's will left Harry everything, including 12 Grimmauld Place and the house-elf Kreacher. Because of Black family pureblood inheritance traditions, there's a risk Bellatrix Lestrange (Sirius's cousin) could inherit instead if Harry can't own it.
- Dumbledore summons Kreacher, who violently refuses ("Kreacher won't, Kreacher won't, Kreacher won't") to serve Harry, wanting his "rightful mistress" Bellatrix. When ordered directly by Harry to shut up, Kreacher nearly chokes — proving Harry is his rightful master. Harry sends Kreacher to work in the Hogwarts kitchens.
- Buckbeak the hippogriff (cared for by Hagrid since Sirius's death) is confirmed to now belong to Harry; Harry lets him stay with Hagrid. He has been renamed "Witherwings" for safety/disguise.
- Dumbledore tells the Dursleys that the blood-protection magic over Privet Drive will lapse when Harry turns 17 (in a year), and asks them to let Harry return once more before that birthday to renew the protection. He bluntly tells them they failed to treat Harry as a son and he's suffered neglect and cruelty.
- They leave; Dumbledore sends Harry's trunk, Hedwig, and cage ahead by magic, but has Harry keep his Invisibility Cloak on him "just in case."

**Named characters:** Harry Potter, Albus Dumbledore, Vernon Dursley, Petunia Dursley, Dudley Dursley, Kreacher (house-elf), Sirius Black (deceased, his will discussed), Bellatrix Lestrange, Buckbeak/Witherwings (hippogriff), Hagrid (mentioned), Regulus Black (Sirius's late brother, mentioned as predeceasing him, childless), Herbert Chorley (mentioned via headline), Augusta Longbottom (quoted), Neville Longbottom (mentioned).

**Named places/objects:** Privet Drive (number four), 12 Grimmauld Place, Gringotts, Hogwarts, the Daily Prophet, Madam Rosmerta's oak-matured mead, Hall of Prophecy, the Ministry pamphlet "PROTECTING YOUR HOME AND FAMILY AGAINST DARK FORCES."

**Specific numbers/details:**
- Harry turns 17 "in a year's time" — wizards come of age at seventeen (Dudley will be 18 "the year after next," Harry is a month younger than Dudley).
- Dumbledore's hand is blackened/shriveled (first shown here; cause not yet explained).
- Harry had sent his "yes" reply by owl.
- The clock showed one minute to eleven before Dumbledore's arrival.

**Obscure/small details:** Dumbledore compliments Vernon's agapanthus flowers. Dumbledore uses a casual wand-flick to make the sofa knock the Dursleys' knees out from under them so they sit. The Dursleys try to avoid drinking the floating mead glasses which nudge their heads insistently. Harry's trunk is nearly empty save for old underwear, sweets, empty ink bottles, broken quills. Harry's alarm clock was repaired by himself years ago.

## Chapter 4: Horace Slughorn

**Plot events (in order):**
- Harry Apparates (side-along) for the first time with Dumbledore, landing in the village of Budleigh Babberton at nearly midnight.
- Dumbledore explains Harry's scar hasn't been hurting because Voldemort has realized Harry has been accessing his thoughts and has begun employing Occlumency against Harry (reversing the usual direction).
- They walk to the home of Horace Slughorn, a former Hogwarts teacher and Head of Slytherin House, whom Dumbledore wants to lure out of retirement to teach again.
- They find the house ransacked, seemingly a struggle; Dumbledore recognizes Slughorn's trick of hiding by magically disguising himself as a piece of furniture (an overstuffed armchair) and reveals him with a wand jab.
- Slughorn restores his house with Dumbledore's help (fixing shattered items via simultaneous spellwork); reveals the "blood" on the walls was dragon blood (decorative/theatrical for scaring off unwanted visitors), his last bottle.
- Slughorn initially refuses to return to Hogwarts; recognizes Harry Potter by his scar.
- Slughorn reveals he was Head of Slytherin, taught Sirius Black (Gryffindor) and his brother Regulus (Slytherin), and taught Harry's mother Lily Evans, whom he adored as a favorite student — a "vivacious," "charming" Muggle-born girl. He also mentions Dirk Cresswell (Muggle-born, now Head of the Goblin Liaison Office), Barnabas Cuffe (editor of the Daily Prophet), Ambrosius Flume (owner of Honeydukes), and Gwenog Jones (captain of the Holyhead Harpies) as former students/contacts whose photos adorn his dresser.
- Slughorn admits he's been living in hiding for a year, moving between empty Muggle houses (freezing burglar alarms with a Freezing Charm), avoiding recruitment by Death Eaters.
- Dumbledore needles him about Dolores Umbridge's fate (expelled from the Forbidden Forest after insulting centaurs as "filthy half-breeds").
- Dumbledore pretends to leave for the bathroom, deliberately leaving Harry alone with Slughorn as bait — Slughorn tells Harry he resembles his father but has his mother's eyes, and muses about Harry being valuable to collect as "the Chosen One."
- Dumbledore and Harry pretend to leave for good; Slughorn capitulates and agrees to teach, demanding a pay rise.
- Dumbledore explains to Harry that Slughorn likes collecting talented/famous/influential people in a "Slug Club," reaping favors (introductions, a box of crystallized pineapple, etc.) and warns Harry that Slughorn will try to "collect" him.
- They Apparate to the Burrow; before parting, Dumbledore speaks privately with Harry in the broom shed, discussing Sirius's death and Harry's grief/resolve, reveals that only two people know the prophecy's full content (Harry and Dumbledore), tells Harry he should tell Ron and Hermione about the prophecy, and announces he will give Harry private lessons this year (instead of Occlumency with Snape). Dumbledore also tells Harry his O.W.L. results would arrive that day and that Harry should keep the Invisibility Cloak on him "even within Hogwarts" from now on.

**Named characters:** Harry Potter, Albus Dumbledore, Horace Slughorn, Lily Evans (Harry's mother, deceased), Sirius Black (deceased, discussed), Regulus Black (deceased, discussed), Dirk Cresswell, Barnabas Cuffe, Ambrosius Flume, Gwenog Jones, Dolores Umbridge (mentioned), Voldemort/He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named, Rufus Scrimgeour (discussed), Amelia Bones (discussed), Cornelius Fudge (mentioned), Hermione Granger (mentioned), Ron Weasley (mentioned, named "Mr. Ronald Weasley" by Dumbledore).

**Named places/objects/spells/creatures:** Budleigh Babberton (village), Hogwarts, Honeydukes, Holyhead Harpies, Goblin Liaison Office, Daily Prophet, Gringotts (implied), Inferi (defined by Dumbledore as bewitched corpses), Occlumency, the Burrow, Apparition/Apparition Test, "Lumos" (spell used by Dumbledore).

**Specific numbers/details:**
- Dumbledore's favorite jam flavor: raspberry (revealed as his security-question answer).
- Slughorn prepared his disguise in "two minutes" (he says), having not heard his Intruder Charm go off because he was in the bath.
- Crystal bottle of dragon blood described as his "last bottle," prices "sky-high."
- Apparition Test requires age seventeen.
- Harry says Sirius was murdered "a few weeks ago" per Slughorn's comment.

**Obscure/small details:** Slughorn wears a maroon velvet jacket with polished buttons over lilac silk pajamas; walrus-like silver mustache. Slughorn's shrunken form means the top of his head barely reaches Dumbledore's chin. He has "pale gooseberry eyes." A spider is seen climbing Dumbledore's hat during the broom-shed conversation — a recurring comic detail. Dumbledore's ring: large, clumsily made gold ring set with a heavy black stone cracked down the middle, worn on his injured hand — Slughorn notices and frowns at it (foreshadowing of the Horcrux ring, revealed later).

## Chapter 5: An Excess of Phlegm

**Plot events (in order):**
- Harry and Dumbledore arrive at the Burrow; Mrs. Weasley and Tonks are up late. Tonks looks drawn, pale, her hair no longer bubblegum-pink; she declines dinner invitations and leaves abruptly.
- Dumbledore leaves for an urgent meeting with Scrimgeour.
- Mrs. Weasley feeds Harry onion soup and bread; reveals Mr. Weasley has been promoted to head the new "Office for the Detection and Confiscation of Counterfeit Defensive Spells and Protective Objects," with ten people reporting to him, due to a flood of fake protective charms/potions being sold amid the panic (mentions Mundungus Fletcher as a likely low-level profiteer).
- The Weasleys' nine-handed clock (each hand names a family member) is shown with all nine hands pointing to "mortal peril" — a running state since Voldemort's return.
- Mr. Weasley arrives home; the family uses a security password routine ("What is your dearest ambition?" — "To find out how airplanes stay up"; "What do you like me to call you when we're alone together?" — "Mollywobbles," revealing Mrs. Weasley's embarrassing pet name).
- Mr. Weasley recounts confiscating fake "Metamorph-Medals" (10 Galleons, cause orange skin color or tentacle warts) and investigating a backfiring jinx incident in Elephant and Castle.
- Harry sleeps in Fred and George's old room (now a joke-shop warehouse); finds a Puking Pastille hidden in the pillowcase.
- Next morning, Ron and Hermione wake Harry. Ginny arrives complaining about Fleur ("Phlegm").
- Fleur Delacour enters with a breakfast tray, announces she and Bill Weasley are engaged to be married; Mrs. Weasley, Ginny, and Hermione are clearly displeased ("Mum hates her," plus Ginny's nickname "Phlegm" for Fleur).
- Conversation reveals: Percy Weasley still estranged from the family; Tonks has been unable to change her appearance (Metamorphmagus powers impaired) since Sirius's death, reportedly due to survivor's guilt over failing to finish off Bellatrix Lestrange in their duel.
- Harry reveals to Ron and Hermione, for the first time, the content of the prophecy: that neither he nor Voldemort "can live while the other survives" — meaning he must ultimately kill or be killed by Voldemort. Hermione accidentally triggers a Fred-and-George trick telescope that gives her a black eye (a tiny fist on a spring pops out when squeezed).
- O.W.L. results arrive by owl. Harry passed everything except Divination and History of Magic, got "Outstanding" in Defense Against the Dark Arts, and "Exceeds Expectations" in Potions (not the "Outstanding" required for Auror training — Harry realizes this likely ends his ambition to become an Auror). Ron got seven O.W.L.s total (more than twins Fred and George got combined). Hermione got nine "Outstanding"s and one "Exceeds Expectations" (Defense Against the Dark Arts) — and is disappointed anyway.

**Named characters:** Harry Potter, Albus Dumbledore, Molly Weasley, Arthur Weasley, Ron Weasley, Hermione Granger, Ginny Weasley, Fleur Delacour, Bill Weasley, Tonks (Nymphadora), Remus Lupin (mentioned), Mad-Eye Moody (mentioned), Fred and George Weasley (mentioned, living above their joke shop), Percy Weasley (mentioned, estranged), Crookshanks (Hermione's cat), Hedwig (Harry's owl), Pigwidgeon (Ron's owl), Gabrielle Delacour (Fleur's sister, mentioned), Mundungus Fletcher (mentioned), Rufus Scrimgeour (mentioned), Bellatrix Lestrange (mentioned, re: Tonks's guilt), Sirius Black (mentioned).

**Named places/objects/spells/creatures:** The Burrow, Office for the Detection and Confiscation of Counterfeit Defensive Spells and Protective Objects, St. Mungo's (implied), the nine-handed Weasley clock, Puking Pastille, Metamorph-Medals, Beauxbatons (Fleur mentions exams after six years of study there), Gringotts (Fleur works there part-time), "The Healer's Helpmate" (book Mrs. Weasley consults, opened to "Bruises, Cuts, and Abrasions"), O.W.L.s (Ordinary Wizarding Levels), N.E.W.T.s (mentioned).

**Specific numbers/details:**
- Mr. Weasley's new office has 10 people reporting to him.
- Metamorph-Medals cost 10 Galleons, promise "a hundred thousand disguises."
- Bill and Fleur have known each other "a year" when they get engaged.
- Ron's O.W.L. total: seven, more than Fred and George's combined.
- Hermione's O.W.L.s: nine Outstanding, one Exceeds Expectations (DADA).
- Harry's O.W.L.s: passed all except Divination and History of Magic; Outstanding in DADA; Exceeds Expectations in Potions (the grade that blocks Auror track, which requires Outstanding).
- Dementors/mist have been present "since the middle of July."

**Obscure/small details:** Mrs. Weasley's pet name is "Mollywobbles." Ron claims to have grown four inches. Ginny's purple Pygmy Puff is later named Arnold. Fleur's sister Gabrielle apparently has a crush on Harry. Hermione reads "Advanced Rune Translation" for fun. The telescope's prank fist is a "Punching Telescope," a Fred and George prototype.

## Chapter 6: Draco's Detour

**Plot events (in order):**
- Weeks pass at the Burrow: Harry/Hermione vs Ron/Ginny two-a-side Quidditch in the orchard.
- News of disappearances/deaths mounts in the Daily Prophet. At Harry's 16th birthday party, Remus Lupin (looking gaunt, grayer) reports Igor Karkaroff found dead in a shack up north with the Dark Mark set above it (deserted the Death Eaters, survived "a year" — contrasted with Regulus Black who "only managed a few days" after deserting).
- Bill Weasley reveals Florean Fortescue (owner of the Diagon Alley ice-cream parlour who used to give Harry free ice cream) has been dragged off/disappeared.
- Mr. Weasley reveals Ollivander the wandmaker has vanished too (shop empty, no struggle, unknown whether voluntary or kidnapped).
- Harry is made Hogwarts Quidditch Captain (gives him equal status to prefects, e.g. use of the prefects' bathroom).
- Trip to Diagon Alley: Mr. Weasley/Bill arrange gold directly from Harry's vault due to lengthy security (goblins tightened security; a man named Arkie Philpott had a "Probity Probe" stuck up him two days earlier).
- Diagon Alley is described as transformed: shop windows covered by Ministry security posters (including Wanted posters of Death Eaters, e.g. Bellatrix Lestrange), shady stalls selling "Amulets" supposedly effective against werewolves, dementors and Inferi.
- At Madam Malkin's robe shop, Harry, Ron, and Hermione encounter Draco and Narcissa Malfoy. Draco insults Hermione ("Mudblood"); jerks his arm away when Madam Malkin tries to touch his left sleeve (foreshadowing the Dark Mark). Narcissa threatens Harry and Ron; references her husband Lucius being in Azkaban and Sirius's death.
- At Fred and George's joke shop, Weasleys' Wizard Wheezes, the group tours an elaborate shop including Skiving Snackboxes (Nosebleed Nougat most popular), trick wands, Patented Daydream Charms, a "U-No-Poo" novelty product mocking Voldemort, a "serious" line of Defense-related products (Shield Hats/Cloaks/Gloves sold even to the Ministry — 500 bought for all its support staff), Instant Darkness Powder (imported from Peru), Decoy Detonators, WonderWitch love potions (effective up to 24 hrs depending on weight/attractiveness), and Pygmy Puffs (miniature puffskeins). Fred and George refuse to charge Harry due to his earlier "start-up loan" gift to them (from Triwizard winnings in book 4).
- Harry, Ron, and Hermione spot Draco Malfoy slipping away alone from his mother and sneak after him under the Invisibility Cloak into Knockturn Alley.
- They witness Draco in Borgin and Burkes demanding shopkeeper Mr. Borgin repair/fix an unnamed object that "has to stay put" (can't be brought to the shop), and reserve another item ("keep that one safe... I'll need it"), threatening Borgin with Fenrir Greyback ("a family friend") if he talks, and swearing Borgin to total secrecy, "including my mother."
- Hermione tries unsuccessfully to pump information from Borgin pretending to shop for Draco's birthday; Borgin throws her out.
- Returning to King's Cross, Harry tells Mr. Weasley his suspicion that Draco has become a Death Eater (based on the sleeve-flinching); Mr. Weasley is skeptical but promises to consider it, noting the Ministry already raided and cleared out the Malfoy house after Lucius's arrest.

**Named characters:** Harry Potter, Ron Weasley, Hermione Granger, Mrs./Mr. Weasley, Ginny Weasley, Remus Lupin, Igor Karkaroff (found dead), Regulus Black (mentioned), Florean Fortescue (missing/dragged off), Ollivander (missing), Bill Weasley, Fleur Delacour, Draco Malfoy, Narcissa Malfoy, Madam Malkin, Fred and George Weasley, Hagrid, Arkie Philpott (mentioned), Bellatrix Lestrange (on Wanted poster), Mr. Borgin, Fenrir Greyback (named, mentioned as "family friend" of Malfoys), Lucius Malfoy (in Azkaban), Sirius Black (mentioned), Crabbe and Goyle (not yet explicitly — appear later), Verity (shop assistant at Weasleys' Wizard Wheezes).

**Named places/objects/spells/creatures:** The Burrow's orchard, Diagon Alley, Madam Malkin's Robes for All Occasions, Knockturn Alley, Borgin and Burkes, Weasleys' Wizard Wheezes, Eeylops Owl Emporium, the Apothecary, Flourish and Blotts, Twilfitt and Tatting's (rival robe shop Narcissa mentions), Skiving Snackboxes, Nosebleed Nougat, Patented Daydream Charms, U-No-Poo, Shield Hats/Cloaks/Gloves, Instant Darkness Powder, Decoy Detonators, Pygmy Puffs, Extendable Ears, the Dark Mark, Invisibility Cloak, Probity Probe.

**Specific numbers/details:**
- Harry turns sixteen this chapter (his birthday party).
- Ministry bought 500 Shield Hats for its support staff.
- WonderWitch love potions last "up to twenty-four hours."
- Harry's vault gold retrieved for him by Bill because public access takes "about five hours" currently due to security.
- Karkaroff survived about a year after deserting the Death Eaters; Regulus Black survived only "a few days."

## Chapter 23: Horcruxes

**Plot events:**
- Harry delivers Slughorn's true memory to Dumbledore late at night; they view it together in the Pensieve: a flashback to teenage Tom Riddle (now wearing the Gaunt ring, implying he's already killed his father) flattering Slughorn in the Slug Club, then privately asking Slughorn directly what a Horcrux is and how to make one. Slughorn explains (reluctantly, uncomfortably): a Horcrux is an object in which a person conceals a fragment of their own soul, split off via the act of murder, allowing the person to avoid death even if their body is destroyed, since part of their soul remains anchored outside the body. Splitting the soul is described as an unnatural violation. Riddle presses Slughorn on whether splitting the soul into multiple Horcruxes (specifically wondering if seven — "the most powerfully magical number" — would make the creator more powerful) is possible; Slughorn is horrified at the suggestion of making more than one and warns him to keep the conversation secret, noting Horcruxes are a banned/forbidden topic at Hogwarts, which Dumbledore polices fiercely.
- Dumbledore explains to Harry: this memory confirms Voldemort was researching how to split his soul multiple times at about Harry's own age. Dumbledore deduces Voldemort made a total of six Horcruxes (not seven — the seventh fragment remains in his own living body, which is why he survived bodily destruction after attacking Harry as a baby).
- Recap/confirmation of known and theorized Horcruxes: (1) Tom Riddle's diary (destroyed by Harry in Chamber of Secrets, confirmed as Voldemort's first Horcrux, created via the murder of Moaning Myrtle); (2) Marvolo Gaunt's ring (found and destroyed by Dumbledore himself in the ruined Gaunt shack — this is revealed as the cause of his blackened, cursed hand; Snape's "timely action" when Dumbledore returned to Hogwarts "desperately injured" saved his life, though the ring's curse could not be fully undone). Remaining, not yet found: Slytherin's locket and Hufflepuff's cup (Dumbledore guesses these are Horcruxes 3 and 4, based on Voldemort's theft from Hepzibah Smith); a possible Horcrux from Ravenclaw or Gryffindor (Dumbledore believes Voldemort wanted relics from all four founders but confirms Gryffindor's known relic, the sword, is safe in Dumbledore's office); and Nagini the snake (Dumbledore's theory for Horcrux #6, created after the Potters, since Voldemort was still short of his target of six when he attacked baby Harry and apparently intended Harry's death to produce his final Horcrux — which failed).
- Dumbledore explains Voldemort didn't pursue a true Sorcerer's/Philosopher's Stone-based immortality (despite trying to steal one 5 years earlier, in book 1) because the Elixir of Life requires endless dependency, which Voldemort — who prizes self-sufficiency — would find intolerable; Horcruxes grant more complete, independent immortality.
- Dumbledore confirms Voldemort never knew the diary (Horcrux #1) was destroyed until he tortured the truth out of Lucius Malfoy years later; Lucius hadn't known the diary's true nature (just that it would reopen the Chamber of Secrets) and acted independently by planting it on Ginny Weasley to frame Arthur Weasley — enraging Voldemort both for losing a Horcrux and for Lucius's unauthorized meddling.
- Dumbledore confirms that once all Horcruxes are destroyed, Voldemort becomes a mortal, killable wizard again, albeit still extremely dangerous due to his intact magical skill and intellect.
- Dumbledore reiterates the "power the Dark Lord knows not" from the prophecy is simply love — Harry's capacity to love, which Voldemort cannot understand or possess, is what let Harry survive repeated contact with Voldemort's mind/possession attempts without harm (explicitly referencing the end of book 5's possession scene at the Ministry).
- Dumbledore delivers his key thesis of the book: the prophecy itself has no inherent power — it only matters because Voldemort chose to act on it, thereby creating his own worst enemy; Harry is free to ignore the prophecy, but will choose to fight Voldemort regardless, out of his own will and love for those Voldemort murdered (parents, Sirius, Cedric Diggory). Dumbledore says this distinction — free choice vs. fated compulsion — matters enormously.

**Named characters:** Harry, Dumbledore, Horace Slughorn (in memory, as young professor), Tom Riddle/Voldemort (in memory, mid-teens), Lestrange and Avery (addressed by Slughorn in the memory), Severus Snape (mentioned re: saving Dumbledore from the ring curse), Lucius Malfoy (discussed), Ginny Weasley (mentioned, re: diary), Arthur Weasley (mentioned), Cedric Diggory (mentioned), Moaning Myrtle (implied victim of the diary's creation-murder, from Chamber of Secrets canon), Phineas Nigellus and other portraits (react with shock/disapproval).

Dumbledore senses the "real" entrance is concealed deeper in using touch and concentration rather than flashy spellwork — identifying this as part of his process for understanding "Voldemort's style" of magic.
- The inner entrance requires a blood payment/toll to open (Dumbledore calls this "crude" magic beneath Voldemort's usual standard) — Dumbledore insists on using his own blood (cutting his uninjured forearm) rather than Harry's, healing the wound instantly afterward, remarking that Harry's blood is "worth more."
- Beyond the archway: a vast underground lake in a huge cavern, with a mysterious greenish glow in the middle marking the Horcrux's location. Attempting a Summoning Charm ("Accio Horcrux") on Dumbledore's suggestion provokes something massive to surface briefly and vanish — confirming guardians lurk in the water.
- Dumbledore locates a hidden chain by touch and reels in a small ghostly green boat (enchanted to carry only one wizard's worth of magical power at a time — meaning it can carry both Dumbledore and the underage, "unqualified" Harry as one passenger's burden). As they cross, Harry spots a human hand, then a full corpse drifting beneath the surface — confirming the lake is full of dead bodies (later identified as Inferi) that remain passive unless the Horcrux is disturbed.
- They reach a small central island holding a stone basin (like a Pensieve) filled with glowing emerald potion. Dumbledore determines the potion cannot be removed, banished, or altered by any means except drinking it — and that drinking it is clearly the intended ordeal for whoever seeks the Horcrux inside. Dumbledore reasons Voldemort would not have designed it to kill the intruder outright, since Voldemort would want to question anyone who got this far and believes himself to be the only one who knows of his Horcruxes.
- Dumbledore extracts Harry's firm promise (reconfirming the deal from ch25) to force him to keep drinking no matter what, even against his will, and no matter what he says or does. Dumbledore then drinks goblet after goblet (twelve total, refilled by magic) of the potion, which induces apparent hallucinations of unbearable guilt, pain, and pleading ("make it stop," "kill me," "I did wrong," protecting unnamed people from being hurt) — Harry, horrified, forces him to keep drinking per his promise, lying that "it isn't real" to calm him.
- After the final goblet, Dumbledore collapses, seemingly near death; Harry's healing spells fail; Dumbledore asks for water. Conjured water vanishes the instant Harry tries to give it to Dumbledore (a deliberate property of the enchantment); realizing only lake water will work, Harry scoops water directly from the lake — which triggers the real guardians: Inferi (reanimated corpses) rise from the water and begin dragging Harry toward the lake to drown him and make him a permanent guardian too.
- Dumbledore, revived by the contact with lake water, summons a massive ring of fire from his wand, driving back the Inferi (who fear fire/warmth) and saving Harry. Dumbledore retrieves the locket-shaped Horcrux from the now-empty stone basin and pockets it. They escape via the boat, cross back through the archway (requiring a second blood toll — Harry provides his own this time, scraping his arm on rock to claim it's an accident), and Dumbledore, extremely weakened, leans on Harry for support out through the sea tunnel.
- Chapter ends with Harry successfully performing his first independent (non-side-along) long-distance Apparition, taking both himself and the badly weakened Dumbledore back toward Hogsmeade.

**Named characters:** Harry, Dumbledore, Tom Riddle (discussed, childhood cave visits), Amy Benson and Dennis Bishop (implied victims, referenced from ch12/25).

**Named places/objects/spells/creatures:** The sea cave (Horcrux hiding place), the underground lake, Inferi (reanimated corpses, confirmed as the lake's guardians), the small enchanted boat (one-passenger-capacity by magical power, not physical weight), the stone basin of emerald potion, "Accio," "Lumos," "Petrificus Totalus," "Aguamenti," the locket Horcrux (apparently Slytherin's locket, retrieved here — though its authenticity is later thrown into doubt).

**Specific numbers/details:**
- Dumbledore drinks approximately twelve goblets of the potion in total before it's fully gone (explicit count goes at least to "the twelfth"/tenth+ mentioned sequentially: text counts fourth, sixth, seventh, ninth, tenth, and more — total appears to be twelve based on the full scene).
- The potion basin is bottomless-seeming but is eventually fully drained.

**Obscure/small details:** Dumbledore identifies the cave by sensing "it has known magic." Dumbledore's healing of his own cut arm mirrors Snape's healing of Malfoy's Sectumsempra wounds in ch24, reinforcing the parallel. Harry instinctively tries multiple defensive spells (Petrificus Totalus, Impedimenta, Incarcerous, even Sectumsempra) against the Inferi, finding only fire effective.

# Cross-Chapter Facts (for franchise-wide ledger)

**Central antagonist/plot device of this book:** Lord Voldemort's Horcruxes — the book's entire narrative backbone is Dumbledore and Harry's private lessons (via Pensieve memories) piecing together Voldemort's life story to identify and locate the magical objects (Horcruxes) in which Voldemort split and hid fragments of his soul to achieve immortality. The book culminates in retrieving a fake Horcrux locket and the murder of Dumbledore by Severus Snape — revealed in this book to be the title's "Half-Blood Prince," author of the mysterious annotated Potions textbook Harry used all year.

**Defence Against the Dark Arts teacher this year:** Severus Snape (finally granted the post after years of Dumbledore's refusal, fulfilling a long-held ambition first revealed in this book to date back to Voldemort's own youth).

**Potions teacher this year:** Horace Slughorn (new/returning staff member, formerly Head of Slytherin House, lured out of retirement by Dumbledore and Harry in ch4).

**Horcrux lore established in this book:**
- Definition: an object in which a wizard conceals a fragment of their own soul, split via the act of murder, to avoid death even if the body is destroyed.
- Voldemort made six Horcruxes total (the maximum Dumbledore believes any wizard has attempted; most wizards never split their soul at all). The seventh fragment of his soul remains in his own body.
- Known/theorized Horcruxes: (1) Tom Riddle's diary — destroyed by Harry in Chamber of Secrets (confirmed in this book as Horcrux #1, created via Moaning Myrtle's murder); (2) Marvolo Gaunt's ring — found and destroyed by Dumbledore in the ruined Gaunt shack (this is what caused his cursed, blackened hand; Snape's intervention partially contained the curse); (3) Salazar Slytherin's locket — stolen from Hepzibah Smith, theorized Horcrux, sought in the sea cave but found to be a fake substitute, with the real one already stolen by a mysterious "R.A.B." before Harry/Dumbledore arrived; (4) Helga Hufflepuff's golden cup — stolen from Hepzibah Smith alongside the locket, theorized Horcrux, not yet located; (5) a theorized but unconfirmed object of Gryffindor's or Ravenclaw's; (6) Nagini, Voldemort's snake — theorized as the final Horcrux, created later in life, since Voldemort was still short of his target when he tried to kill baby Harry.
- A Sorcerer's/Philosopher's Stone-based Elixir of Life was rejected by Voldemort as a path to immortality because it requires ongoing dependency, which he (prizing self-sufficiency) found intolerable.

**Memories shown in the Pensieve this book (chronological, in-world):** Bob Ogden's memory of the Gaunt family (ch10); Caractacus Burke's memory of buying Slytherin's locket from Merope (ch12); Dumbledore's own memory of meeting 11-year-old Tom Riddle at the orphanage (ch12); Dumbledore's memory of teenage Riddle visiting the Gaunt shack and framing Morfin for murder (ch17); Horace Slughorn's doctored memory of teenage Riddle asking about Horcruxes (ch17, revisited true version in ch23); Hokey the house-elf's memory of Riddle visiting Hepzibah Smith and her cup/locket (ch20); Dumbledore's own memory of adult Voldemort requesting the DADA job years later (ch20).

Gryffindor beats Slytherin in the opener (helped by Harry's trick with fake Felix Felicis on Ron). Gryffindor loses badly to Hufflepuff, 320-60 (verified against book text: "three hundred and twenty to sixty"), when Harry is knocked unconscious by a Bludger mishit by substitute Keeper Cormac McLaggen, costing Harry a cracked skull and the match. **Named spells introduced/featured prominently this book:** Sectumsempra (Snape's invented slashing curse, confirmed as his own creation at the book's climax), Levicorpus/Liberacorpus (Snape's invented hex that lifts a victim by the ankle, and its countercurse), Muffliato (Snape's invented privacy-noise spell), the Half-Blood Prince's "Elixir to Induce Euphoria" tweak. Other spells/charms featured: Protego, Expelliarmus, Petrificus Totalus, Episkey, Aguamenti, Specialis Revelio, Relashio, Langlock, Anapneo, Diffindo, Reparo, Oppugno.

**Deaths in this book:** Albus Dumbledore (murdered by Severus Snape atop the Astronomy Tower, via Avada Kedavra, at the climax — this is the book's central, major death). Also: Aragog (Hagrid's giant Acromantula spider, dies of natural causes/old age); the Death Eater Gibbon (killed by friendly fire during the final battle); Charity Burbage (former Hogwarts Muggle Studies teacher, murdered by Voldemort himself for writing pro-Muggle-born articles, shown in the book's opening chapter among the Death Eaters — technically the very first scene of the book, set before Harry's narrative begins); an unnamed five-year-old boy (the Montgomery sisters' brother, killed by a werewolf attack rumored to be Fenrir Greyback, mentioned in ch22); Hepzibah Smith (wealthy witch, poisoned by Voldemort decades earlier, revealed via memory in ch20); the Riddle family — Tom Riddle Sr. and his parents (murdered by Voldemort decades earlier, revealed via memory in ch17).

**Central antagonist/plot device recap for ledger:** Horcruxes (Voldemort's soul-splitting objects) and the mystery of the Half-Blood Prince (revealed to be young Severus Snape) are the book's two interlocking plot engines, both resolving around Snape, who kills Dumbledore at the climax after having spent the whole series as a trusted (if hated-by-Harry) double agent.

**Other notable facts for cross-book tracking:**
- Harry's parents' deaths are described in more detail than before: Voldemort killed James first, then gave Lily the choice to step aside (she refused, protecting Harry), establishing the "ancient magic"/love-based protection that saved Harry as a baby.
- The book establishes that Snape overheard only the first half of Professor Trelawney's prophecy about Harry and Voldemort (at the Hog's Head, interrupted/caught eavesdropping by the bartender), and brought word of it to Voldemort, directly causing the Potters to be targeted — Snape's defection to Dumbledore's side is tied to his guilt over Lily Potter's resulting death.
- Dumbledore's thesis on free will vs. prophecy: the prophecy itself holds no power except that which Voldemort gave it by acting on it; Harry is free to choose whether to pursue Voldemort, but his own feelings (love for his parents, Sirius, Cedric) would compel him to regardless.
- The book ends with Harry breaking up with Ginny Weasley to protect her, and declaring his intention to leave Hogwarts for good to hunt the remaining Horcruxes, setting up the final book's plot directly.
